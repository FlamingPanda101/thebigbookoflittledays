### 🎨 The Main Event: Cardboard Box House
**🧰 Needs:** 1 large box · box cutter (adult only) · paint or markers · tape
1. Cut a door and one or two windows. Let her point to where before you cut.
2. Hand it over. She paints and decorates however she likes — resist suggesting a chimney.
3. Add a paper-plate clock or a taped-on mailbox if she wants details.
4. **Leave it standing all week.** Repeat play is where the value is.

> 💡 **Tip:** Cut it *before* she's watching. Three-year-olds have strong opinions about box modifications and it's easier to negotiate with a finished door.

### 🌳 Get Outside: Boot-Print Trail
Make a deliberate trail of prints — snow, mud, frost, wet pavement — then follow each other's tracks back to the door.

> 👶 **Kreston (7 months):** Prop him in the doorway with a large safe toy, a few minutes at a time. New enclosed spaces are fascinating at this age and he wants to be where his sister is.

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight — The cardboard box beats the expensive toy, and there's a reason.**
> A toy that does one thing teaches a child to operate it. A toy that does nothing asks the child to supply everything — and that's where the cognitive work happens. When she gets bored of a fancy toy in four minutes and plays with the box for an hour, nothing has gone wrong. She's choosing the richer option.
