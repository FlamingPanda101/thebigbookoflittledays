Mother's Day & Family Love 💐

May 7 – May 13, 2027 · Days 127–133

This week points Azlyn at the four people in her own house. She photographs them, feeds them, makes a toy for Kreston, mends what belongs to them and puts clean sheets on their beds. A family is a set of people you do things for, and she is old enough now to be one of the ones doing.

Mother's Day lands on Sunday. Saturday builds toward it, she lays the table for five and guards the plate in the fridge, and Monday winds back down out of it into the button tin and a shoebox of things nobody throws away. The handprint card she paints on Sunday is May's, fifth of this year's twelve, so hold April's sheet up beside it and put both back on the wall.

🧒 What Azlyn will learn
  • What a frame leaves out. She lines up every shot through a cardboard window first, so choosing one thing means dropping another.
  • What it takes for two things to be the same. A tin of odds and ends hunted for exact twins, and every near miss argued out with a reason she has to say out loud.
  • Making something for a particular person. The plate, the card, the cake and Kreston's rattle all get judged on whether they land with the person they were for.
  • Hand strength with a point to it. Cracking an egg, beating batter until it holds on the fork, pushing wool through a needle eye, pulling a stitch through from her side.
  • Carrying her own share of the work. A wet laundry basket across the grass, two full watering cans, a pillow shoved into its case.

Kreston is 11 months this week. He stands alone for a second or two before he sits down hard, cruises anything at hip height, and babbles in strings all day. He stops when you start, which is him taking his turn.

Her Turn With the Camera

⏱️ 60 min🧰 5 items

You need: 1 old phone or any digital camera (if you have neither, your own phone with the wrist strap round her wrist), The cardboard viewfinder from 8:00, 1 roll masking tape, 1 permanent marker, 1 sheet of paper for her list of twelve

  1. Tape a strip of masking tape across the back of the camera and write her name on it in marker. If you are lending your own phone, wind the strap twice round her wrist and tape the loose end down.
  2. Show her the button once, then take your hands off it. She lines each shot up through the cardboard frame first, drops the frame, and shoots.
  3. Number the sheet of paper one to twelve. Twelve photographs of the things in this house she wants to keep.
  4. After each one, ask why that. Write her answer on the numbered line word for word, wrong grammar and all.
  5. Somewhere in the twelve every person in the house has to appear, you included. Stand where she puts you.
  6. Go through all twelve on the screen at the end while she reads her own list back off your handwriting.

💡Straighten nothing and move nothing out of shot. The dishwashing in the background is half of why the photograph will be worth having in ten years.

The World From Kreston's Height

Six spots in the house, three heights at each, eighteen photographs. Kreston lives in a different version of this room.

⏱️ 90 min🧰 5 items

You need: The same camera or phone, Masking tape, 6 pieces to mark the spots, 1 permanent marker, 1 sheet of paper, One of Kreston's toys

  1. Walk the downstairs together and pick six spots: the kitchen doorway, under the table, the bottom stair, wherever she says. Press a piece of masking tape on the floor at each and number them one to six in marker.
  2. Sit Kreston's toy on the floor at spot one. Kneel down and hold the camera at the height of his head for the first photograph.
  3. Same tape, same spot, and she takes the second one standing at her own eye height.
  4. For the third she stretches the camera as high as her arms go, or you lift her up and she shoots from there.
  5. Work through all six spots that way. Tick each height off on the paper as you go, because eighteen photographs run together fast.
  6. Sit down with the screen and give her three shots of one spot out of order. Let her say whose is whose before you tell her.
  7. Hunt for the toy in all three. It fills the frame from Kreston's height and you can hardly find it from yours.

💡Kneeling gets old by the third spot. Sit on the floor and let her be the one going up and down, and keep her thumb clear of the lens.

🧠 One thing worth knowing

Tools shape what a child notices.

A camera puts a frame round the world, and a frame is an instruction to choose. Azlyn has walked past this kitchen every day of her life. Hand her something that takes one rectangle at a time and she starts hunting for what belongs inside it. A magnifying glass does the same thing to a paving slab, a flashlight does it to a cupboard, a jam jar does it to a puddle. The tool decides which question she asks. Twelve is a small enough number to hurt, so she has to leave things out. Read her twelve reasons back to her in December.

⏱️ 90 min🧰 6 items

🧠 One thing worth knowing

One of the oldest child-centered teaching rituals we still have.

At Passover, a few weeks back this spring, the youngest child at the table asks the Four Questions and the rest of the meal answers them. The tradition is getting on for two thousand years old and it is built around a child who asks. Azlyn asking four questions tonight sits in that line. She holds the floor, the table answers her, and a question of hers stops a room. Three-year-olds ask hundreds of questions a day and get most of them half-answered on the move. Keep the cards. Ask them again at Christmas, and again when she is seven, and listen to the answers change.

⚠️

Safety: Small round foods on an open plate with an 11-month-old at the table: grapes and cherry tomatoes quartered lengthways, no whole nuts, no olives with pits in, and nothing left within reach of the high chair. The scissors stay with you.

⏱️ 60 min🧰 7 items

The Cake She Ices Herself

Hand this page to Joseph. Both children go into the kitchen, the door shuts, and Brooklyn does not come in for ninety minutes.

⏱️ 90 min🧰 6 items

You need: 1 plain sponge or a store-bought loaf cake (plain cookies work the same way), 1 3/4 cups powdered sugar, 2–3 tbsp water, Food coloring, Sprinkles or chopped fruit, 1 bowl, 1 spoon, 1 butter knife, 1 plate

  1. Powdered sugar into the bowl. Azlyn tips it and half of it goes on the counter.
  2. Water in one spoonful at a time while she stirs. Stop when it falls off the spoon in a ribbon and holds its shape for a second.
  3. Food coloring, one drop, her choice of color, and she stirs it through. Two drops if the first disappoints her.
  4. Stand the cake on the plate. She spreads the icing with the butter knife, thick in some places and bare in others, and nobody smooths it after her.
  5. Sprinkles or chopped fruit go on by the fistful. Do not ration or arrange them.
  6. Twenty minutes for the icing to set. She washes the bowl and the spoon in the sink while it does.
  7. She carries it out on the plate when she can no longer stand waiting.

💡Keep a couple of spoonfuls of powdered sugar back in the bag. A spoonful stirred in fixes any mix that has gone too runny.

🧠 One thing worth knowing

Rituals that repeat annually do something one-off events can't.

Azlyn has no working idea of how long a year is. A thing that happens once a year, remembered from last time and expected next time, is how a year turns into a real unit for her: the handprint card, the same walk, the capsule on the shelf. The second time is when it starts working, and the fifth time it belongs to her rather than to you. Keep this one small enough to survive a bad May. One sheet of card, two hands and six answers takes forty minutes, and forty minutes is a tradition you can still manage in 2031.

Nothing goes in Kreston's mouth or hands. Washable non-toxic paint only, and the washcloth is in your hand before the paint touches him.
