VERDICT
==============================================================================
Days 183-364 are not ready for design. The bones are strong: the outdoor calendar is specific, Kreston has real jobs, most Safety blocks are actively written rather than boilerplate, and the voice holds for 182 days. But three things have to be fixed before a file goes to layout. First, safety: two days carry a real hazard with nothing on the page at all (D300 has no Safety block; D304 sends them onto residential roads after dark with no walk rule), the lap-slide is recommended three times, and the book contradicts its own too-big-to-swallow rule on five separate object classes within days of stating it. Second, the US conversion stopped somewhere around December: July through November still ask Brooklyn to buy squash, cornflour, lining paper, A4 and things priced in pounds, still send her out for conkers and sycamore spinners, and still run a British sunset clock and a British DST date across seven days. Third, the brief's own named recurring defects all recur here: a step contradicting its Safety block (four days), water with no adult-contact sentence (five days), a hazard named with no action (three days), pairs of days on one shopping list, insight overclaiming, and Azlyn written as three on four of the eleven days after her fourth birthday. None of it is structural and none of it needs a rewrite of the book, but the safety and conversion passes are edits to roughly seventy days, and design should not start against the current files. Everything below the must-fix line is texture: real, worth a pass, not print-blocking.

STRUCK: 8 findings
Struck as taste or as explicitly out of scope. (1) The identical-title claims (D254/D297 'The Dig', D238/D308 'Opening Day') and the retitling asks that followed them - the brief lists duplicate titles as automated-verified; I kept only the duplicated-activity substance underneath. (2) Tip-opener frequency counts ('Let her' x32, 'She will' x25, 'Do not' x18) - style statistics, not something a reader catches as wrong. (3) The broad 'do it more than you think' tip-template complaint - kept only the one genuine case, D184 and D193 giving the identical tape sentence nine days apart. (4) Capping chalk at one titled use a week - chalk is the correct outdoor answer often enough that this is a preference, not a defect. (5) The 'walk the house and collect a category' opening format across twenty days - same reason. (6) The 'The Same X, N Times' title formula - taste. (7) The three tone complaints (D188, D320, D348 tone half) - the pages read as on Brooklyn's side; the D348/D287 near-copy-paste survives separately as a real duplicate. (8) D243's missing sandpaper substitute - a nitpick on a page that already has bigger problems.

PER-DIMENSION VERDICTS

SAFETY: Not ready. The typical block is specific, age-aware and matched to its steps, and the work on button batteries, open flame, hot pans and frozen metal is genuinely strong. But D300 has no block at all on the day the week's choke-sized objects are pooled onto one tray, D304 covers the pumpkin and not the after-dark walk, sun cream is absent from the two hottest months, three gutter days have no traffic sentence, four days have a step instructing what their own block forbids, and five water moments lack the arm's-reach sentence the book writes well everywhere else. Every one of these is an edit rather than a rewrite, but there are about twenty of them and they are the highest-consequence findin

MATERIALS: Not ready. The lists are honest household-and-recycling lists and the substitutions are usually generous, but the US conversion clearly ran out of steam: July through November still ask for squash as a drink, cornflour, lining paper, A4 sheet counts, punnets, double cream, flapjack and British money, and every recipe is grams-only including the one titled 'One Cup Means One Cup'. December (roughly D339 on) is cleanly American, which is what makes the rest look unfinished rather than deliberate. Add the script wreckage ('cotton balls balls', 'an popsicle') and this is one sweep that has to happen before layout.

REPETITION: The weakest dimension after safety. Twenty-six weeks were visibly written blind to each other: consecutive-day duplicates (D241/D242 stick walk, D270/D271 first leaf fall), pairs from one shopping list two to three weeks apart (D211/D235 tent, D247/D262 map, D190/D206 foil boat), three ice-cube days in eleven, four gutter-water days in seven weeks, seven pretend-shop days, and eleven separate 'write her words down exactly' tips. Not all of it is print-blocking - I struck the chalk, opening-format and tip-template complaints as taste - but the tight clusters are, because Azlyn will say so out loud and Brooklyn will feel the book running out of material at the point in summer she most needs it

INSIGHTS: Good voice, defensible content, badly repetitive and numerically wrong in one class. Fifteen days quote roughly an hour of sustained single-activity attention when ten to twenty minutes is typical at three, and D194 states the opposite in the same book - that is the number Brooklyn measures herself against and it recurs often enough that she cannot dismiss it. D212's fever headline is the one claim that could change behaviour and is false. Four days still call Azlyn three after her birthday. Underneath that, fourteen pairs of insights make the same argument twice, one pair (D287/D348) with a near copy-paste headline whose bodies then disagree.

INFANT: Genuinely good arc, undermined by one systemic contradiction. Kreston gets real jobs rather than being scenery, and the month-to-month milestones mostly land. But the book breaks its own too-big-to-swallow rule on five object classes within days of stating it - ice, bottle caps, cotton balls, conkers, stones - and the lap-slide is recommended three times, which is a fracture mechanism the book would police if it were a bottle cap. Below that: two afternoon templates cover about forty of his 182 days, four settled skills are re-announced as new, and his attention spans are overclaimed the same way Azlyn's are.

SEASON: Strong month-by-month weather sense and almost every Get Outside is a specific named activity rather than filler, but a British layer was never lifted out. Conkers on eight days, sycamore spinners and ash keys, a blackberry window that runs August to late October and contradicts itself, a sunset clock an hour early on seven days and the wrong DST date, double-decker buses, pounds and 50p, lidos and allotments. D355 is the sharpest single failure: the solstice day cannot be run on frozen ground and offers no fallback. Everything here is fixable by recomputation and substitution, but it touches roughly forty days.
