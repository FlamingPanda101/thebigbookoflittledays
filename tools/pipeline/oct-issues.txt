--- major | oct-weather-1 · Main Event: The Race Down the Glass
PROBLEM: The experiment does not work from where Brooklyn is standing. Step 1 has her wipe the INSIDE of the pane dry, so the cloth is on the inside; the raindrops she is racing run down the OUTSIDE. Step 5 then says 'Wipe a dry stripe down the middle of the pane with the cloth and race a drop on the dry side against a drop on the wet side.' Wiping the inside changes nothing about the outside drops, and the Safety block keeps the window catch shut, so she cannot reach the outer face. The change-one-thing step, which is the point of the hour, fails the first time she tries it.
FIX: Replace step 5 with a variable she can control from inside: race a fat drop against a small one and have her call the winner before they start, or tape a strip of masking tape flat on the inside of the glass across the track and let her watch how a drop behaves crossing it. Alternatively move the whole activity to the shower screen or a baking tray propped at an angle with a spray bottle, where she owns both faces of the glass. Also drop 'the inside of the glass' from the materials line so the cloth is not implied to be an experimental tool.

--- minor | Section intro and oct-extras · Lamps On at Five
PROBLEM: The opening paragraph says 'the lamps are on by five,' and the extras page carries '**Lamps On at Five** — she walks the house switching every light on as the dark arrives.' Daylight saving runs to November 7 in 2027, so October sunset sits between roughly 6:45 PM on the 1st and 6:00 PM on the 31st. At five o'clock it is still daylight across most of the country for the whole month. The related craft line, 'the street at five o'clock, lit windows drawn in white and yellow on a dark sheet,' inherits the same problem. This is November's hour, not October's.
FIX: Move the hour later and hang it on the rain rather than the clock. Intro: 'the lamps go on in the afternoon.' Extras: rename to '**Lamps On Before Dinner**' with 'she walks the house switching every light on as the gray comes down.' Change the crayon entry to 'the street after dinner, lit windows drawn in white and yellow on a dark sheet.'
