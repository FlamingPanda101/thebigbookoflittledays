# 🌧️ May — Backup Days & Extra Ideas

May is the month that promises and then goes back on it. You get a warm
morning, a black sky by eleven, thunder over lunch and sun again in time for a
long light evening. These pages are for the days the plan stops working: two
full days for when the weather shuts you in, two for when one of them wakes up
ill, and a page of ten-minute ideas for the mornings you open this book with
nothing left in the tank. Nothing here needs a store trip, because on those
days you are not going to one.

---

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 1 — Thunder and Nowhere to Run

For the day the storm sits over the house. Warm air, rain hitting the glass
sideways, thunder near enough to feel, and a three-year-old with a full tank
and no yard to empty it into. The whole plan below is built to burn that fuel
indoors and then bring her down again after lunch.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Stand the paper recycling in a pile by the sofa, put the masking tape and a marker on top of it, and empty the laundry basket. If the storm is forecast, tell her at bedtime that tomorrow is an inside day, so the news is old by breakfast.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: Fifty Paper Balls
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Sticky Doorway (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Loudest Window in the House (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: Rain Down the Paper (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🤸 The Jumping Line Down the Hallway (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: The Paper Balls Back in the Basket
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Painted Sheets, Now Dry
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Warm Washcloths and Pajamas Early
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Fifty Paper Balls

**🧰 You need:** the paper recycling, newspaper and junk mail · 1 laundry basket · 1 marker · masking tape.

1. Sit on the floor with the pile between you and show her one ball: a sheet crushed in two hands until it stops making noise. Loose ones fly wrong, so squeeze yours hard where she can see it.
2. Count them into the basket out loud as she goes. Fifty is the target and thirty is a working morning.
3. Write the number on a strip of tape and stick it to the basket. She will beat it before lunch and want the number changed.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Sticky Doorway

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 roll masking tape or packing tape
- 1 doorway with nothing breakable through it
- The basket of paper balls
- 1 marker
- 2 strips of tape for the throw line

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Run four strips of tape across the doorway, sticky side facing her, at heights between her knees and the top of her head. Press the ends to the frame and press them hard. Keep the lowest strip above Kreston's standing reach.
2. Stand her one long step back and put a tape line on the floor there. Give her ten balls and let her throw. Some stick, most drop, and the sound of the ones that stick is the whole appeal.
3. Count what stuck. Write the number on the door frame in marker or on a strip of tape beside it.
4. Now hand her the roll and let her change something. More strips, a wider gap, the line moved back, tighter balls. One change at a time is the rule, and you say it once rather than enforcing it.
5. Throw the same ten again and compare the counts. She will want to argue about which change did it. Let her, and write both numbers up.
6. Take a turn yourself from the back of the room. Losing to her is worth more than coaching her.

> 💡 **Tip:** Tape that has gone dusty stops catching. Peel that strip off, throw it away and run a fresh one, rather than letting her decide she has broken it.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Loudest Window in the House

Nobody is going out in this. Take the movement anyway. Go around the house
window by window, stand a step back from each one for a count of ten, and rank
them by how loud the rain is. Run between them rather than walking. The windows
stay shut and the front door stays closed while you can hear thunder, and both
of you keep off the glass and the frames. Finish at the loudest one and count
the seconds between the flash and the rumble together. A long count puts the
storm a long way off, and a count that grows each time means it is leaving.
The open door waits for the end of the day. Twenty minutes after the last
thunder, step onto the mat with her and let her smell what May rain does to hot
ground, because that smell is one she will recognize for the rest of her life.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 11 months and thunder will wake him from
> the morning nap. Bring him down rather than settling him back up there on his
> own. Wet paper turns to a plug in a baby's throat, so the ball basket lives on
> the table between throws and the floor gets swept before he goes down on it.
> Give him a second basket with a folded blanket and three books in the bottom,
> because an empty one tips the moment he pulls up on the rim. He hauls himself
> up on the weighted one, holds the stand for a second or two, and drops back
> onto his bottom, which is the work his legs need this month. At the windows,
> hold him a step back from the glass and say **rain** every time. One word said
> the same way twenty times is how it stops being noise to him.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: Rain Down the Paper

The morning was loud and fast. This one is slow, sitting down, and it makes
something that stays on the wall.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- Washable poster paint in two or three colors
- 3 small cups or a muffin tin
- Water, and 1 teaspoon per color
- Thick paper or cut-open cereal boxes, 4 sheets
- 1 baking sheet or a large tray
- Masking tape
- 1 old dish towel

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Mix each color in its own cup with enough water that it runs off a spoon in a thread. Too thick and it sits still, which is the one way this fails.
2. Tape a sheet of paper to the baking sheet at all four corners. Rest one end of the sheet on a rolled dish towel so the paper sits at a slope.
3. Spoon one blob of paint along the top edge and stop. Both of you watch it decide to move.
4. Her turn with the spoon, one color at a time, along the top edge only. The rule she has to hold is that the brush stays out of it. Gravity does the work.
5. Lift the tray higher and let her see the runs go faster and thinner, then lower it and watch them stall and pool. Ask her which one looks more like the window.
6. Second sheet, and let her break the rule she learned an hour ago. Tilt it sideways, tip it end over end, blow one run off course.
7. Lay the finished sheets flat on the kitchen counter to dry, up out of Kreston's reach, and leave them there. She will go back to them at 4:15 and find the pools have dried with hard edges.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do your own sheet on the other side of the tray. She works longer next to somebody working than next to somebody watching.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Skip the paint and race two real drops down the window glass, best of five, from a step back.
- Flatten the morning's paper balls back out and draw on the creases.
- Tape a sheet to the fridge and do the same runs standing up.
- Give her a cup of water and a dry brush and let her paint the front door with it.

### 🤸 The Jumping Line Down the Hallway

The paint held her still for ninety minutes and the storm is still going, so
the hour after it belongs to her legs. Lay one strip of tape across the hallway
floor for the takeoff and let her jump from it. Mark where she lands with a
second strip and write the number of the jump beside it. She goes again, aiming
past her own mark, and the marks pile up down the hall. Two-footed, one-footed,
backward, from a run-up: the variation is hers to invent and yours to lose at.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Watered-down paint goes in his mouth the moment
> your back turns, so he needs his own thing at the same table height. Two inches
> of warm water in a roasting pan on a towel, one cup and one spoon, and he will
> stay with it for twenty minutes. He tips it over himself at the end. That is
> the point at which you move him, not before.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A system she can change one piece of beats a toy that already works.**
> A toy that works gives her one move, which is to switch it on. The doorway
> you taped up this morning gave her ten. She moved the throw line, packed the
> balls tighter, aimed at the low strip because it caught more, and every change
> she made showed up in the count. That loop is engineering at three, and she can
> run it on tape and old newspaper. Watch for the moment she starts changing one
> thing at a time instead of everything at once. It shows up around now, and it
> is worth more than any score on the door frame. Leave the tape up an extra day
> if she is still testing it.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Tape strung across a doorway at head height is a hazard for a
> baby who is pulling up on furniture. Kreston does not go through that
> doorway while it is up, so gate it, shut it, or keep him in the room with
> you. Every strip comes down and goes in the trash the moment the game ends,
> and the floor gets swept before he crawls on it. Wet paper balls and torn
> tape both choke. During a thunderstorm keep both children off the corded
> phone, out of the bath and away from the sink until the thunder has been
> gone twenty minutes or more. Fill the paint cups, the roasting pan and the
> washcloth bowl in one trip, and let the dishes wait. The day ends with warm
> washcloths and pajamas early instead of a bath. No open windows and no open
> front door while you can still hear it.

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 2 — The Rain That Sets In

May does this too. Gray from breakfast to bedtime, eight degrees colder than the
forecast said, and rain that never gets going enough to be interesting. The cold
snap buys you a day indoors, so spend it on the job the warm weather has been
waiting on, which is getting the summer clothes out and the too-small ones
gone.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Pull the bag or box of hand-me-downs and next-size clothes out of the closet and stand it by her bedroom door. Two empty laundry baskets, a marker, and the full-length mirror uncovered if you have one.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: Three Piles on the Bed
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Show Down the Hallway (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Test Run in the New Clothes (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: Kreston in Her Old Clothes (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🤸 The Fastest Outfit in the House (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: The Hangers Back on the Closet Rail
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Empty Bed and the Hand-Me-Down Bag
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Bath and the Pajamas She Chose
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Three Piles on the Bed

**🧰 You need:** her drawers and hanging clothes · 1 bed or a cleared floor · 2 laundry baskets.

1. Empty every drawer she owns onto the bed while she watches, then bring what hangs in her closet down on top of it. The size of the heap is half the fun and she needs to see it all at once.
2. Three piles, named out loud as you start: **fits me** stays on the bed, **too small** goes in one laundry basket and **not yet** in the other. She rules on each item and you hold your tongue on the strange calls.
3. Anything she cannot decide stays in the middle of the bed for 9:15. Aim for a middle pile of eight or so, because that is an hour.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Show Down the Hallway

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The undecided pile from the bed
- The bag of next-size hand-me-downs
- 1 full-length mirror, or the bathroom mirror
- Masking tape for the runway line
- 1 marker and 4 strips of paper
- Your phone for photos

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Run a line of masking tape down the hallway floor and tell her it is the runway. The rest of the house watches from the kitchen doorway.
2. She puts on one thing from the undecided pile. Getting dressed on her own is most of the work here, so sit on the floor and wait rather than helping. Help arrives when she asks for it.
3. Down the runway and back, then a look in the mirror. She gives the verdict, keep or pass on, and you write it on a strip of paper and put the item on the matching pile.
4. Two rules that make it honest: arms up over her head, and squat down to the floor. Anything that pulls tight in either of those has gone too small, and she can feel that for herself.
5. Work through the whole pile, then open the hand-me-down bag and do the same with what is in there. Photograph the outfits she loves, because these are the clothes she will wear in every picture this summer.
6. Give her the drawer at the end and one instruction: what fits goes in, and it has to shut.

> 💡 **Tip:** She will want to keep something that has not fit her since March. Let her keep it. One item costs you nothing and it buys you agreement on the other twenty.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Test Run in the New Clothes

She stays in whatever she chose. Take the movement break in it and call it the
test. Ten laps of the downstairs, a stair climb, a crawl under the table, arms
up high enough to touch the door frame. Anything that rides up, drops down or
stops her doing something gets pulled out of the drawer and put back in the bag.
Open the front door and stand on the mat to watch the rain for a minute at the
end. Cool air on bare arms is information too, and she can tell you herself that
today is not the day for it.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 11 months and a bed covered in soft
> piles is the best thing he has seen all week. Put him on it with you and let
> him pull the heaps over. He stands himself up against the mattress edge, holds
> it for a second or two and sits back down, over and over. Say the word for each
> thing you hand him, one word, the same one: **sock**, **hat**, **shoe**. Keep
> drawstrings, plastic bags and any string longer than his forearm off the bed
> and in the closet.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: Kreston in Her Old Clothes

The morning was about her body getting bigger. The afternoon hands the evidence
to somebody smaller, which is the part she has been waiting for.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The **too small** pile
- 1 clean towel or blanket on the floor
- 1 laundry basket or a bag for what he cannot use yet
- 1 marker and masking tape for labels
- Your phone, with old photos of Azlyn on it
- Kreston, in a good mood, after a nap

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Spread the too-small pile out on the towel and let her pick five things for Kreston. Her choices, including the ones that will swamp him.
2. Find a photo on your phone of Azlyn wearing one of them and show her before you start. She will not believe it was her.
3. Dress him in one, with her doing the arms and you doing the head. Say the plan out loud to him each time, because eleven months is old enough to hold an arm out for a sleeve when he knows what is coming.
4. Photograph each one on the same spot on the towel. Two of the five will fit him now and the rest go in the basket with a tape label she scribbles on.
5. Let her tell him what he looks like. He will hand her something back and wait for her to take it, which is his newest trick and worth stopping for.
6. Load the rest of the too-small pile into the bag and write the size on it big enough for her to point at.
7. Stand the bag by the door for whoever is getting it, and let her be the one to put it there.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stop at five, even when she wants to keep going. He will run out of patience before she does, and the afternoon is better ended on a good one.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Try on your own clothes instead and let her rule on those.
- Line up every pair of shoes in the house, smallest to biggest, and count.
- Sort the whole pile by color instead of by size and photograph the rows.
- Wash the four things she loves most by hand in the sink so they are dry by morning.

### 🤸 The Fastest Outfit in the House

She has been sitting down since breakfast and the yard is out, so give the hour
to her legs and to the drawer she filled this morning. She picks a whole outfit,
lays it on the floor in the shape of a person, and stands at the end of the
hallway. You count out loud. She gets the outfit on, runs the hall and back, and
you tell her the number she landed on. Three rounds, a different outfit each
time, and the socks are what will beat her. Take a turn yourself in something of
your own and let her count you down.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Give him a drawer of his own on the floor, filled
> with the things from the bag he cannot wear yet. Pulling soft items out of a
> container one at a time and dropping them holds an 11-month-old longer than any
> toy in the house. Sit him next to Azlyn's pile rather than in it, so she keeps
> her order and he keeps his job.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **She'll defend a category harder than she'll defend a fact.**
> Ask her whether a shirt still fits and you get an answer. Put that shirt on
> the **fits me** pile and the answer hardens into a position she will hold
> against the evidence of her own arms. Sorting does that at three, and it is
> the reason the arms-up test earns its place: it moves the argument off her
> word and onto her body, so she can change her mind without losing. Let her
> name the piles and let her be wrong in one or two of them. She will re-sort
> half of it herself over the next week, on her own time with nobody watching,
> and that version is the one that holds.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Sort the drawstrings out today while everything is in front of
> you. Cords and ties at the neck of a hood strangle a crawling baby on furniture
> and playground equipment, so cut them out of anything going to Kreston. The
> plastic storage bags go straight back to the closet the moment they are empty.
> Old shoes stay out of the hand-me-down bag: a walker needs a sole that has not
> already been worn into somebody else's shape.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1 — Flat All Day

The day she does not get off the sofa. Fever, a throat, a stomach bug, it makes
no difference to the shape below. There are no times on this page on purpose.
She sleeps when she sleeps and eats when she asks, and the whole job is to keep
the day small and let the illness run. Nothing here needs setting up, cutting or
cleaning after, and every piece of it can be dropped mid-sentence when she
turns her face into the cushion.

### 🌙 Before She Wakes
> Strip the sofa of anything that cannot be washed and build the nest: her pillow, two blankets, the bucket, a pile of washcloths, a full cup with a straw in it. Put a towel under the whole thing. Clear the coffee table down to nothing but the cup, and cancel today out loud to yourself before she comes down, because the plan you had is over.

### 🕰️ The Shape of the Day
- **Whenever she wakes** — down to the nest in whatever she slept in
- **Before she asks** — the first drink, small and cold, on the table beside her
- **The first long stretch** — 🛋️ Nothing But the Nest
- **When she surfaces** — 🪟 The Window From Where She Is
- **When she asks for food** — dry, plain and half of what you think
- **The long middle** — 😴 The Rest Block
- **After the sleep** — 🧼 The Warm Cloth and the Cold Cloth
- **The flattest hour, late afternoon** — 📖 The Story of Kreston's Morning
- **Before dinner she may not eat** — 🎵 Two Songs and a Phone Call
- **Early, and without the usual routine** — bed

### 🛋️ Nothing But the Nest

The first stretch is the one where you do the least. She lies down, you sit at
her feet with your hand on her leg, and the television goes on if that is what
gets you both through it. Guilt about the screen costs you energy you need for
the rest of the day. Keep the room dim, keep the house quiet, and keep the cup
where her arm reaches without her sitting up. Say once that she is allowed to
stay there all day, and then prove it by not asking her to move.

### 🪟 The Window From Where She Is

**🧰 You need:** the sofa turned or her head at the window end.

1. Turn her so she can see out without lifting her head. That one move is worth more than any activity on a bad day.
2. Give her a job with no effort in it: tell me when a car goes past, tell me when the tree moves. May weather does the rest, and a gray sky that breaks into sun over an hour is a whole show.
3. Answer her with a word. She wants company at this end of the day and a sentence is more than she can hold.

### 😴 The Rest Block

This is the middle of the day and the point of the day. Two hours, curtains
half drawn, no screen, no talking, no visitors. She sleeps or lies there, and
both count. Lie down yourself if Kreston is settled or somebody else has him,
because you will be up in the night with her and this is the only chance you
get. If she fights it, sit beside her with your hand flat on her back and say
nothing at all. A silent adult is what tips a wired sick child over the edge
into sleep.

### 🧼 The Warm Cloth and the Cold Cloth

**🧰 You need:** 2 washcloths · 1 bowl of warm water · 1 bowl of cool water.

1. Warm cloth first, over her face, hands and the back of her neck. Slow, and the same order every time, because the order is the comfort.
2. Cool one folded on her forehead after, if she is hot. She will hold it there herself and that is the first thing her hands have done all day.
3. Fresh pajamas at this point if she can face it. A clean top on a sick child buys an hour of better mood.

### 📖 The Story of Kreston's Morning

**🧰 You need:** your voice, and her eyes shut.

1. Tell her what her brother did while she was lying there, in order and out loud: he woke at six, he banged the spoon on the tray, he ate half the cereal and dropped the rest, he cried at the vacuum.
2. Get one thing wrong on purpose in every stretch. She corrects you from the cushion without opening her eyes, and the correcting is the whole activity.
3. Keep going after she stops answering. She is still listening for a good while after she stops talking.

### 🎵 Two Songs and a Phone Call

Put on the two songs she asks for and leave them on repeat. Then call somebody
who loves her, put it on speaker, and hold the phone near her without making
her perform. A grandparent talking to a silent child for four minutes gives her
the feeling of being visited and costs her nothing. End it before she has to be
polite.

> 👶 **Kreston Today:** He is 11 months, cruising, and he will head for her
> face, her cup and her bucket in that order. He needs somewhere else to be for
> most of today. Call whoever can take him for the afternoon, and if nobody can,
> gate one room, put the sofa out of reach and run the day from there. Everything
> she has touched is his next: her cup goes in the dishwasher after every use,
> her cloths go in a bag, and you wash your hands between the two of them all
> day. He will get it anyway, four days behind her. Feed him and settle him
> somewhere Azlyn cannot hear the two of you.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Illness is a good time to be the one with the ideas rather than the hands.**
> A sick day strips out everything she does with her body. Her legs are no use
> to her, her hands last four minutes, and the part of her still running at full
> speed is the part that thinks. Give that part the work. Ask what happens next
> in the story, which song, which blanket, what the cup should have in it, and
> then do the moving for her. She gets to run an afternoon from a cushion and
> you get a child who feels like herself instead of a patient. Talk less than
> usual while you do it. One question every few minutes is plenty and the
> silence in between is part of the rest.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Fluids beat food by a mile, so keep small cold drinks going all
> day and count her wet diapers or trips to the bathroom. Call the doctor the
> same day for: no wet diaper in eight hours, a dry mouth with no tears, a fever
> over 102°F that will not come down, or a child too sleepy to wake for a drink.
> Call an ambulance for a stiff neck, a fit, hard fast breathing with the skin
> pulling in between her ribs, blue or gray lips, or a rash of small dark spots
> that stays visible when you press a glass against it. Medicine goes back on the
> high shelf between doses, and you write down the time of every one, because the
> day blurs and you will not remember.

## 🤒 Sick Day 2 — Done Lying Still

Day three, or day four. The fever has gone, she is done lying still, and she is
not well enough for anything real. This is the hardest day of the illness for
you, and the answer is small jobs with a visible end rather than free play she
has not got the fuel for. Still no times, still no going out, and the rest block
in the middle stays even though she will tell you she does not need it.

### 🌙 Before She Wakes
> Move the nest from the sofa to the floor by the window, which changes the room enough to buy an hour. Fill the cup, put a banana and some crackers where she can reach them, and pick three of the jobs below to have ready. Not four.

### 🕰️ The Shape of the Day
- **Whenever she wakes** — breakfast on the floor by the window, whatever she wants
- **The good hour, and it comes early** — 📚 The Books in a Line
- **When she flags** — back on the cushions with no comment from you
- **When she asks for food** — 🍊 The Peel That Doesn't Break
- **The long middle** — 😴 The Rest Block, Shorter and Still Not Optional
- **The slump after** — 🧺 Ten Things in the Wrong Room
- **When she starts talking about tomorrow** — ✏️ The Five Drawings for Next Week
- **Late, when nothing else is left** — the bath, long and early
- **Bedtime** — the usual routine, back in place, on time

### 📚 The Books in a Line

**🧰 You need:** every book in the house she can reach.

1. Bring the lot to her in armfuls and dump them where she is sitting. She does not fetch today.
2. One rule, chosen by her: tallest to smallest, or the ones she loves at one end and the rest at the other. Her rule, and she has to say it out loud before she starts.
3. Leave her to it and go do something in the same room. Come back when the line is done and walk its whole length while she tells you the order.

### 🍊 The Peel That Doesn't Break

**🧰 You need:** 1 orange or a tangerine · 1 plate.

1. Start the peel with your thumbnail and hand it over. The whole job is getting it off in one piece.
2. She will break it. Start a second one and tell her that two out of three is a good result at this.
3. Lay the peels on the plate and let her build the fruit back up out of them. Eating some of it is a bonus on a day like this.

### 😴 The Rest Block, Shorter and Still Not Optional

An hour, in the afternoon, in her bed rather than on the floor. She will argue
harder today than she did yesterday, and the argument is proof she needs it,
because a child on the mend who skips this crashes at four and takes the evening
with her. Books in with her, no screen, curtains half shut. Sleep is a bonus and
lying down is the whole requirement.

### 🧺 Ten Things in the Wrong Room

**🧰 You need:** 1 laundry basket · your legs.

1. Walk the downstairs with the basket and collect ten things that live somewhere else. She picks them from where she sits, pointing.
2. Tip the basket out in front of her. She sorts them into piles by the room each one belongs to.
3. You do the walking and she does the counting. Ten back where they belong, and the empty basket is the finish line she can see.

### ✏️ The Five Drawings for Next Week

**🧰 You need:** 1 sheet of paper · 1 marker · the fridge.

1. Ask her for five things she wants to do the week she is better, and take them one at a time.
2. Draw each one on the sheet yourself, stick figures, numbered one to five. Hold the sheet up after every drawing so she can tell you what you left out.
3. Tape it to the fridge at her height. She crosses one off with the marker each day next week, and the swimming or the park goes first.

> 👶 **Kreston Today:** He is 11 months and he has spent two days shut out of a
> room he can hear her in. Bring him back in this afternoon, on the floor, with a
> border of cushions between the two of them and a basket of his own things.
> Azlyn will want him closer than is wise, so give her a job that involves him
> from a distance: she names what he picks up and you hand it to him. Keep the
> cups separate and keep her hands off his face. He may be starting his own
> version of this in a day or two, and a rested adult handles that better than a
> spotless kitchen does.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A job with a visible end is what a recovering child wants.**
> Boredom on the third day is a sign the illness is loosening, and it turns up
> before her stamina does. Open play needs energy she has not got back yet, so it
> falls apart into whining within minutes and you both end up worse off. A job
> with edges holds. Twelve books to line up, one orange to peel, ten things to
> send back to their rooms: she can see the finish from the start, and reaching
> it hands her the thing this week has taken away, which is evidence that she can
> still do something. Keep them small enough to finish in one sitting. A job she
> abandons costs her more than no job at all.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** A child on the mend goes back to preschool or a friend's house
> 24 hours after the last vomiting, diarrhea or fever, with no medicine holding
> the fever down. Going back early spreads it and buys you a relapse. Watch for
> the second wave: a fever that clears and returns two days later, a cough that
> gets worse rather than better, or a child who goes quiet again after a good
> morning. Call the doctor for any of those. Kreston's cups, spoons and pacifiers
> stay separate from hers for the rest of the week.

## 🎲 May — Extra Ideas

For the morning you open this book with ten minutes and no plan. One line each,
no steps, nothing that needs buying.

### 🎲 Alternative Activities

- **The Storm Tally** — one mark on the door frame paper per rumble, read out at dinner.
- **Dandelion Clocks** — one puff each, then count what is left on the stalk.
- **After-Dinner Walk** — the long May light spent on a slow lap of the block in pajamas.
- **Barefoot on Three Floors** — grass, tile and carpet, and her verdict on each one.
- **Maple Helicopters** — two dropped off the step at once and she calls the winner.
- **The Hose With Her Thumb On It** — ten minutes of watering, and her thumb doing the work.
- **Ants at the Crack** — one crumb left on the path and checked every half hour.
- **The Sound Under the Umbrella** — standing still in the rain to listen to it hit.
- **The Wet Grass Sprint** — shoes off, fence to door and back twice, feet dried on the mat.
- **The Stairs Counted Two Ways** — numbers going up, the same numbers backward coming down.
- **The Cupboard Lunch** — one shelf, and she picks the whole meal off it.

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts

- **The Dandelion Rub** — yellow heads rubbed hard on paper until the color comes off.
- **The Grass Brush** — a fistful of grass taped to a stick and used as a paintbrush.
- **Tape Resist Letters** — her initial taped on paper, painted over, the tape peeled off last.
- **The Egg Carton Caterpillar** — six cups, twelve legs and a name she picks.
- **Petal Prints** — petals folded inside paper and tapped with the back of a spoon.
- **Cardboard Sunglasses** — cut from a cereal box with colored plastic wrap for lenses.
- **String Painting** — a paint-soaked string dragged out from between two folded sheets.
- **The Foil Flower** — foil pressed around a bottle cap and bent out into petals.
- **The Junk Mail Collage** — flyers and envelopes torn up and glued into one shape.
- **Handprint Wings** — two hands printed either side of her name down the middle.
- **Wax Rain** — white crayon lines pressed hard, then blue paint washed over the top.

### 👶 Kreston's Turn

- **The Low Shelf He Can Empty** — one shelf cleared and refilled with things he may pull out and drop.
- **Cruising for a Cracker** — the snack set at the far end of the sofa, with the furniture in between.
- **One Word All Day** — a single object named the same way every time it comes near him.
- **The Roasting Pan of Warm Water** — two inches of water on a towel, one cup, and you within arm's reach.
