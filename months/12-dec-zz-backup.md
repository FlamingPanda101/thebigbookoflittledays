# 🌧️ December — Backup Days & Extra Ideas

These are the December days the plan cannot survive. Ice on the sidewalk,
snow coming down sideways, dark by four thirty and a car you would rather
not move, or a kid on the sofa who wants nothing except you sitting near her.
Two full indoor days sit here, two sick days that ask almost nothing of
either of you, and a page of one-line ideas for the morning you open this
with ten minutes and no plan. Take one and put the week's page back in the
drawer until tomorrow.

---

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 1 — Hot Water on the Coldest Morning

Snow came down all night, the steps are under it, and the cold has the car
doors frozen shut. Nobody is going out. The warmest thing in the house today
is a sink of water at her height, and she gets to stand in it up to her
elbows for as long as she wants. Put a towel down first and the whole day
costs you one wash load.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Push the step stool to the sink, fold two old towels on the floor in front of it, and stand the dish soap and a scrub brush where she can see them.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: The Basket of Grubby Things
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Sink at Her Height (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Ten Trips Without Spilling (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: The Sock Mountain (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: Putting the Dry Cups Away
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Last Bowl of Suds
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Warm Bath, Warm Room
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: The Basket of Grubby Things

**🧰 You need:** 1 laundry basket · her · every room in the house.

1. Give her the basket and one rule: anything that goes in it has to survive a soak. Plastic plates, cups, toy animals, blocks, the tea set, a doll with hair you can dry.
2. Walk the house with her. She decides. Take the wooden spoon out if she picks it and tell her why, then let her put something else in its place.
3. Stand the full basket by the sink and tell her the water goes on at 9:15. She will ask four times before then, so give her a job in the meantime: line the dirtiest ones up along the counter, worst first.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Sink at Her Height

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 step stool, steady, pushed tight against the cupboard
- The sink filled a third full, plus 1 dish pan or big bowl of clean rinse water
- 1 teaspoon dish soap, no more
- 1 scrub brush and 1 sponge
- 2 old towels for the floor, 1 dish towel for the clean plastic and her hands
- 1 tray or drying rack for the clean ones
- The basket of grubby things

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Run the water to warm on your own wrist before she gets on the stool. She tests it after you, and she never touches the faucet.
2. Show her the order once: wash in the sink, rinse in the bowl, stand it on the tray. Then step back and let the order fall apart.
3. Hand her the brush for the ridged things and the sponge for the flat ones. She will use the brush for everything, which is fine.
4. Once the foam is gone she washes with gray water and does not care. Tip it, refill it warm, and let her count how many things one bowl of soap gets through.
5. Ask her to find the dirtiest thing left in the basket and do that one next. Naming it turns the last twenty minutes into a hunt.
6. Dry the plastic together on the dish towel and carry the tray to the table. She put a stack of dirty things through and came out with a stack of clean ones, and the tray is the proof.

> 💡 **Tip:** One teaspoon of soap. A squeezed bottle gives you a sink of foam with no water under it, and the foam is what she plays with instead of the job.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Ten Trips Without Spilling

The walk is off, so the movement has to come from somewhere. Set a full cup
of warm water on the counter and an empty bowl at the far end of the hall,
and send her back and forth. Ten trips, counted out loud, walking flat and
slow. The bowl fills a finger at a time and she learns that fast hands empty
the cup before she gets there. Race her on trip eight. Lose.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 18 months and wants the sink he cannot
> reach. Set his own dish pan on a towel on the kitchen floor, an inch of
> warm water, one cup and one plastic animal. Pretend play has started, so
> he will wash the animal because he watched Azlyn wash hers, and he will tell
> you about it with the words he has. Stay in the room the whole time, and tip
> the bowl out the moment he walks away from it.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Sock Mountain

Morning was wet to the elbows. This afternoon is dry, warm and sitting down,
and it ends with every sock in the house paired for the first time since
August.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- Every clean sock in the house, out of every drawer
- 1 laundry basket, emptied onto the rug
- 1 small basket or bag for the odd ones
- 4 paper bags, or 4 plastic grocery bags, one per person
- 1 marker for the names
- Her, the rug and a warm room

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Tip the lot into a heap in the middle of the rug and let her get into it. The heap is half the appeal and it costs you nothing.
2. Start with the easy rule: hers or not hers. Two piles, and she can tell by size before she can tell by pattern.
3. Move to pairs. Hold one up, she hunts the match. Say the pattern out loud as you go, stripes and spots and the gray ones with the hole.
4. Anything without a partner goes in the odd basket. Tell her the partner might turn up, and mean it.
5. Roll each pair together. Her rolls will come apart in the drawer, and they go in as they are.
6. Write a name on each bag with the marker while she watches you write it, then sort the pairs in. She reads the names off the first letter, and Kreston's bag she knows by size.
7. Deliver the bags room by room. She empties each one into its drawer, and the job is finished in a way that morning jobs almost never are.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not re-pair the ones she got wrong. A blue sock married to a green one is her decision, and correcting it in front of her ends the sorting for the afternoon.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Pair her socks only and leave the adult heap for another day.
- Line the odd socks up longest to shortest and stop there.
- Stuff four odd socks with the others to make a soft ball each.
- Read in the sock heap, which is warmer than it sounds.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** He is 18 months and posting things is close to his
> favorite thing. Hold his named bag open and let him drop socks in, then
> carry a rolled pair to a room and hand it over. He has twenty-some words, so
> ask him whose it is before you tell him, and wait through the pause. Keep him
> out of the heap while Azlyn is working in it or the pairs come apart faster
> than she makes them.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Doing the dishes is water play with a result, and she'll do it for the water alone.**
> Hand her a bowl of warm water on a housebound morning and she stays at it
> for forty minutes, which no craft you set up this month has managed. Warm
> water on skin settles a small body, the same reason a bath rescues a bad
> evening, and the sink adds weight, resistance and a job with a visible end.
> She is running a real experiment on how much soap makes how much foam. The
> plates come out clean or they do not, and either way she has moved a stack
> of dirty things into a stack of clean ones at adult height, doing adult
> work. Put the towels down before she climbs up and stay within arm's reach.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Set the water warm on your own wrist first, and she never
> touches the faucet. No knives, glass, graters or anything with a blade goes
> into the wash, and check the sink with your hand before she puts hers in.
> Kreston's bowl holds one inch and gets tipped out the second he leaves it,
> because a toddler can drown in less water than that, and he is never in the
> kitchen with standing water and no adult. Wet tile is slippery, so the towels
> stay down all morning and shoes come off.

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 2 — The Floor Is the Job

Freezing rain over the last of the snow, black ice on the steps, and a
four-year-old with a full tank and nowhere to empty it. Today she works with
her feet. She polishes the hallway, climbs the stairs a dozen times, and by
lunch she is worn out in the way a cold walk would have worn her out.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Put four old towels and two pairs of thick socks by the hall door, and clear the hallway floor of everything with a corner on it.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: Which Floors Slide
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: Polishing the Hall on Two Towels (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Everything That Lives Upstairs (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: Everybody's Feet in a Row (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: The Boots Lined Up by the Door
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Paper Feet
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Lights Low, Feet Up
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Which Floors Slide

**🧰 You need:** 2 old towels · thick socks · every floor surface in the house.

1. Towel under each foot, hands free, and walk her onto the kitchen tile. She slides. Let her fall over twice before you say anything about it.
2. Take her to the carpet and try the same thing. Nothing moves. Then the hall, then the bathroom, then the rug by the door.
3. Ask her which floor is the fastest and let her rank all of them out loud. She picks which end of the hall gets the turning post, and you set it there at 9:15.

### 🎨 The Main Event: Polishing the Hall on Two Towels

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 4 old towels, two dry and two damp
- Thick socks, hers and yours
- 1 spray bottle of water, or a bowl to dip a corner in
- Masking tape for the start line, or a folded dish towel laid across it
- 1 empty laundry basket as the turning post
- A clear stretch of hard floor, nothing underfoot

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Stand the basket at the end of the hall she picked at 8:00 and tape a start line at the other. That is the course, and the walls are out of bounds.
2. Wet two towels with the spray bottle, or dip a corner in the bowl and wring them out. Thick socks on both of you, one damp towel under each of her feet. She pushes from the start line to the basket, around it, and back, and the floor behind her comes up clean.
3. Send her over the same strip four or five times. Skating is the fun part and the cleaning happens whether she means it or not.
4. Swap to the dry towels for the buffing run. Tell her the job now is shine, and let her hunt for the streaks she left.
5. Load her up: hand her something to carry across on the return trip so her arms work as well as her legs.
6. Finish with one slow run where she balances with nothing to hold, then look down the hall together with the lights on low. The floor is different because of her, and she can see it.

> 💡 **Tip:** Damp, not wet. A soaked towel puts water on the boards and turns a fun morning into a slick you have to mop before anybody walks it.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Everything That Lives Upstairs

Fill the basket with everything downstairs that belongs on another floor:
laundry, books, a hairbrush, three cups, a stray boot. She carries it up one
armful at a time and puts each thing where it lives. Count the trips out loud
and let her decide when she is done. Stairs are the best climbing frame in
the house on a day like this, and by trip six she is breathing hard.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 18 months and climbs stairs holding on,
> one foot at a time, so he goes up with the others. Stay one step below him,
> hands off, and let it take as long as it takes. Downstairs, give him a dry
> washcloth of his own to push around on hands and knees while Azlyn skates,
> and keep him well off the polished stretch while she is moving on it.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: Everybody's Feet in a Row

Morning ran through her legs. This afternoon her feet stay still and get
drawn around, and the year shows up in the size of them.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- Flattened cardboard, cereal boxes or a roll of paper, enough for six outlines
- 1 pencil and thick markers
- Blunt scissors for her, heavy scissors for you (**adult only**)
- Masking tape
- Every pair of shoes and boots by the door, plus the summer ones from the closet

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. She stands on the cardboard with her sock off and you draw around her foot. Hold the pencil upright and expect her to giggle and move.
2. Do everybody: yours, hers, Kreston's, anyone else in the house. Write the name inside each outline while she watches you write it.
3. Cut them out, you on the thick card and her on the easy edges. Names face up.
4. Lay them on the floor smallest to biggest. She will want hers next to yours, so let her hold both up and compare them face to face.
5. Dig out her summer sandals and stand them on her outline. They do not fit any more, and this is the first time she has seen a whole year as a measurement.
6. Tape the row along the baseboard at her height, biggest at one end. Write the month beside each one.
7. Let her try on every shoe by the door, including yours, and walk the polished hall in them.

> 💡 **Tip:** Draw around the foot rather than the shoe. A shoe outline is a shoe, and a foot outline with her name in it is her, which is the reason she keeps looking at it.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Her foot and your foot, cut out, taped up, done in fifteen minutes.
- Trace hands instead, and put them beside January's handprint sheet.
- Measure the hall in her feet, heel to toe, and write the number on a card by the door.
- Draw around her whole body on flattened boxes and let her color it in.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** He is 18 months, and pretend play is running now,
> so he will put your boots on and take four steps before he goes over. Sit
> beside him and let him do it. Name each shoe as he tries it and wait for his
> version of the word. His outline goes in the row too. Stand him on the
> cardboard, hold one hand so he keeps still, and draw fast.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Doing a job with her feet is how she gets her whole body into it.**
> Ask a four-year-old to sit and color on a housebound day and you get
> eleven minutes. Give her a job that runs through her feet and you get an
> hour. Pushing, bracing and sliding send steady pressure through her joints,
> and that load settles a body with nowhere to run, which is the whole trouble
> with a dark December morning. Her feet carry her weight, so a foot job pulls
> in her legs, hips and back at the same time, and the work is heavy enough to
> matter. She finishes worn out the way a cold walk would have worn her out.
> Hand her the towels, name the start line and stay out of the route she
> picks.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Towel skating happens on a clear straight run with no stairs
> at either end and no glass, no radiator corner and no table leg in the path.
> Close the stair gate before she starts. Damp towels only, and mop any water
> she leaves before the next run. On the stair trips, stay one step below
> Kreston with your hands free, and send one child at a time. The heavy
> scissors are yours and go back on a shelf between cuts.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1 — The Day She Runs It From the Sofa

This is the flat day. She has a fever, she wants you in the room, and she
will not stay with anything for longer than four minutes. Nothing here needs
cutting, baking or a cleanup she has to be part of, and you can drop any of
it halfway through without wasting a thing. Your whole job today is fluids,
rest and being close by.

### 🛋️ Set Up Once, Before Anything Else
> Sofa nest, a cup with a lid on the floor beside it, a bowl within reach, a towel over the cushion, and the thermometer and medicine on a high shelf. Build it once and you will not have to get up for the rest of the morning.

### ⏳ The Shape of the Day
- **Whenever she wakes** · straight to the sofa, not the table. Water first, food later or not at all.
- **After the first long sleep** · one quiet thing on her lap, ten minutes, then let it go.
- **When she asks for food** · dry toast, a clementine, a yogurt. Anything she names is the right answer.
- **The long middle** · the rest block. Nothing is scheduled against it.
- **Before the afternoon dip** · a drink, a wet cloth on her forehead, the curtains closed.
- **When it goes dark at four thirty** · lamps on low, television if that is what gets you both to six.
- **Whenever she has had enough** · bed early, and take the win.

### 😴 The Rest Block

The center of today is a long stretch where nothing is asked of her. Two
hours, maybe three, on the sofa or in your bed, curtains half closed and one
lamp on. She may sleep and she may lie there watching the ceiling, and both
count. Sit within reach with your phone or a book. A feverish four-year-old
who cannot find you spends her energy checking where you are, so stay in the
room and let her feel you there. Line Kreston's nap up with this block and
you get the quietest ninety minutes of the day.

### 🛋️ Building It Where She Points

**🧰 You need:** every cushion and blanket in the house · her voice · your arms.

1. Ask her where the nest goes and take the answer, whether it is the sofa, the floor or the corner behind the armchair.
2. She names each piece and where it lands. You carry, you fold, you tuck. Do it wrong once and let her correct you.
3. Once she runs out of instructions, ask where the next thing goes rather than filling it in yourself.
4. The nest stays up until she is well, so nothing here goes back tonight.

### ✏️ The Picture She Orders Up

**🧰 You need:** paper on a book · 1 pencil or crayon · her lying flat.

1. Tell her you will draw anything she says, and she does not have to lift her head.
2. She talks, you draw. A dog with six legs comes out as a dog with six legs.
3. Ask her one question per picture: what color, how big, what is it standing on.
4. Write her title along the top in her words and put it where she can see it from the sofa.

### 📖 The Story You Get Wrong

**🧰 You need:** a story she knows by heart · her eyes shut.

1. Tell it from memory with her lying down and the lamp off.
2. Get one thing wrong on purpose every few lines. The wolf knocks on the wrong door, the bear eats the porridge cold.
3. She will correct you flat on her back with her eyes closed, which is the most she can give today and it costs her nothing.

### 🎯 If She Will Not Settle
- Move the whole nest to your bed and start again there.
- One long audiobook or a familiar movie, with you on the sofa beside her.
- A cool washcloth on the back of her neck and the window cracked for one minute.
- Name every animal she can think of, out loud, taking turns until she stops.

> 👶 **Kreston Today:** He is 18 months, he wants to climb on her, and he cannot
> be in that room unsupervised for a second. Move his nap earlier so it lands
> inside her rest block. If somebody can take him out for two hours, today is
> the day to ask. Keep him going with things that need no setup from you:
> stairs with you one step below, his own basket to empty, a spoon and a bowl
> and a toy that gets fed. Separate cups, and wash your hands between the two
> of them.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Directing an adult is the strongest evidence of competence she can get.**
> Flat on the sofa she has lost every job she owns. She cannot carry, climb,
> pour or fetch, and a four-year-old measures herself with those. Directing
> you hands it back. She names the cushion, the corner, the color of the
> blanket, and an adult moves. That loop tells her she still has an effect on
> the world on a day her own body has stopped cooperating with her. Follow her
> instructions to the letter, including the ones that make no sense, and give
> it back to her when she stalls by asking where the next one goes. She is
> doing the thinking and you are supplying the arms, and she knows the
> difference.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Fluids matter more than food today. Small sips often, and
> count the wet diapers or trips to the bathroom. Call your doctor for a fever
> that will not come down, a rash that does not fade under pressure, breathing
> that looks like hard work, a stiff neck, refusing to drink for hours, or a
> child you cannot rouse. Medicine goes back on a high shelf between doses,
> never on the sofa arm or the floor, and write the time of each dose on a
> piece of paper. No hot drink within her reach or Kreston's.

## 🤒 Sick Day 2 — Bored and Not Better Yet

Day three or four. The fever has gone, she is fed up, and she cannot manage
anything you would call a day. This is the harder one to get right, because
she will demand more than she can handle and then fall apart at two o'clock.
Give her a smaller version of normal and keep the rest block, even though she
says she does not need it.

### 🛋️ Set Up Once, Before Anything Else
> Leave the sofa nest up. Put a lap tray or a big book beside it, a cup with a lid, and a bowl of clementines and whatever else is sour in the fridge.

### ⏳ The Shape of the Day
- **Whenever she wakes** · she will be up before you want her to be. Sofa, water, and no expectations for an hour.
- **Once the first hour is behind you** · one thing on the lap tray, sitting up, twenty minutes at most.
- **When she asks for food** · say yes to whatever she names. Appetite coming back is the news of the day.
- **Before the afternoon dip** · the rest block, and hold the line on it.
- **After the rest block** · the one small job, which is the whole point of today.
- **When it goes dark at four thirty** · lamps low, and no visitors.
- **Bedtime** · earlier than she thinks she needs, because tomorrow is the day she overdoes it.

### 😴 The Rest Block

She will argue about this one. Do it anyway: an hour on the sofa after lunch
with the curtains half closed and something she has heard forty times playing
low. The rebound day is where families lose the recovery, because a child who
feels better at eleven is back on the sofa by three and back to square one on
Thursday. Say it out loud as a rule rather than a suggestion. Lie down with
her if that is what makes it happen.

### 🍋 One Sour Thing at a Time

**🧰 You need:** a clementine · a lemon wedge · plain yogurt · a pickle or a spoon of cranberry sauce · 1 saucer. With no citrus in the house, an apple wedge and a cracker with a drop of vinegar on it do the same job.

1. Put them on a saucer on her lap and let her go in whatever order she likes.
2. Try each one yourself, out loud, before you say anything about hers.
3. Ask her to put them in order, most sour to least, and take her order as final.
4. Come back to the lemon at the end. Most children go back a second time once the surprise has gone.

### 🍊 Peeling for the Whole House

**🧰 You need:** 4 or 5 clementines, or any fruit in the house with a skin she can get off · 1 bowl for the peel · her lap.

1. Start each one for her with a thumbnail and hand it over. Peeling is slow work with one hand and she has all day.
2. One segment for each person in the house, laid out on the tray in named piles.
3. Let her deliver them, or call people over if she is not up to standing.
4. Keep the peel in the bowl on the radiator. The room smells of it for the rest of the afternoon.

### 📝 The Card That Says What's Next

**🧰 You need:** 1 index card or a piece of thick paper · 1 marker · her ideas.

1. Ask what she wants to do on the first proper day back and write it down word for word.
2. Cap it at three things. A recovering child who plans six wakes up owing herself six.
3. She draws one small picture beside each line, or tells you what to draw.
4. Tape it where she can see it from the sofa and read it back whenever she asks.

### 🎯 If She Is Climbing the Walls
- Sofa to the window and twenty minutes of naming what goes past.
- Everything in one drawer emptied onto the tray and put back in a new order.
- A phone call to somebody who will let her do the talking.
- Ten minutes of her leading and you following, room to room, at her pace.

> 👶 **Kreston Today:** He comes back in short doses. He is 18 months and full
> of pretend play, so give him a spoon, a bowl and a toy that needs feeding,
> and set him up on the rug where she can watch without being climbed on.
> Twenty minutes together, then move him on before it goes wrong. He may well
> be the next one down with it, so keep the cups separate and expect a broken
> night. If he has been shut out of her room for two days, he will want you,
> and ten minutes of your full attention buys you the afternoon.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Sour is the taste she'll learn to like on purpose, and that's a new experience.**
> Sour reads as a warning to a small child, and a four-year-old spits out
> lemon on reflex. Then she watches you eat a wedge, tries it again, and
> something switches over. Learning to like a taste on purpose is a new kind
> of experience for her, and she is the one running it. A mouth coming off
> three flat days is dulled, and sour cuts through where sweet slides past, so
> this is the week it lands. Give her a clementine, a lick of lemon and a spoon
> of plain yogurt and let her rank them. If her throat is still raw, leave the
> lemon out and come back to it in a few days.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Keep the fluids going, because a child on the mend drinks
> less once she stops being watched. Citrus stings a raw throat and a cracked
> lip, so let her stop the moment she says so. Kreston gets clementine segments
> broken in half with the seeds out and the pith pulled off, and he sits up to
> eat them with you next to him. Whole segments, grapes and lemon rind are all
> choking hazards at 18 months. If she gets worse again after a day of
> improving, call your doctor rather than waiting it out.

## 🎲 December — Extra Ideas

### 🎲 Alternative Activities
- **Both Sides of the Front Door** · one hand flat on the inside of the door, one on the storm door glass, and she calls the difference.
- **Paper Snowball Fight** · a stack of newspaper crumpled into ammunition, the sofa as the wall.
- **The Practice Wrap** · a shoebox, a sheet of leftover paper and as much tape as she wants to use.
- **Four O'Clock Lights Off** · every lamp off at the moment it goes dark outside, then one back on at a time.
- **Guess Which Hand** · a small thing hidden in one fist, three guesses, then she hides and you guess.
- **The Cupboard Orchestra** · a pan, a wooden spoon and a jar of rice, played loudest to quietest.
- **The Bed Picnic** · lunch upstairs on a towel, and nobody goes back down until it is finished.
- **Heavier or Lighter** · two cans, one in each hand, and she calls it before the scale does.
- **Freeze When It Stops** · three rounds of music and stopping, then she works the button and you freeze.
