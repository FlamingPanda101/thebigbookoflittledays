# 🌧️ December Backup Days
## *For when the plan doesn't survive the morning*

**December's version of a bad day isn't usually the weather.** It's the house being full of people, the routine being gone, everyone being overtired, and a four-year-old who has had too much of everything for a fortnight. The two "bad weather" days below are really bad-*week* days — for when it's dark and wet and the plan has no chance, and for the strange flat stretch between Christmas and New Year when nobody knows what day it is.

The sick days matter this month too. Something always goes round in the last week of December, and it always arrives at the worst possible moment.

---

<a id="dec-weather-1"></a>

## 🕯️ Bad Weather Day 1: The Dark and Wet Day
**Use when:** It's black by four, pouring, and going outside is genuinely not happening.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting: Turn the Lights Off (15 min)
- **9:15–10:30 AM** — Main Event: The Shadow Theatre (75 min)
- **10:30–10:45 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:45–11:30 AM** — Indoor Movement: The Shadow Chase (45 min)
- **11:30 AM–12:00 PM** — Quiet Play (30 min)
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch & Nap Prep

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Shadow Theatre
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A white sheet, or a pale blank wall
- A strong torch, or a lamp with the shade off
- Black card, scissors, and lolly sticks or straws
- Tape
- The peg dolls and the storybook from Week 49
- A dark room

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Hands first, with nothing else.** Torch on the wall, lights off. **A dog, a bird, a rabbit.** Ten minutes of this before anything gets made.
2. **Then the discovery that runs the whole morning:** move her hand towards the light and the shadow gets **bigger**. Move it away and it shrinks. **Let her find it and then test it fifteen times.**
3. **Cut puppets from black card** — the characters from her Week 49 story — and tape each to a stick.
4. **Rig the screen:** sheet pinned across a doorway, torch behind, audience in front.
5. **Run her story from Week 49 as a shadow play.** She already knows the plot, so nothing has to be invented today.
6. **Then the scale trick on purpose:** a small puppet held close to the light becomes a giant on the screen. **That's how she makes the monster.**
7. **Swap sides** and let her be the audience while an adult performs badly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Sheet in a doorway with the torch on the floor behind it is the setup that works. Torch held in a hand wobbles, and the wobble is what ends the play.

### 🏃 Indoor Movement: The Shadow Chase
Torch on the floor, everyone standing. **Try to step on each other's shadows.** Then freeze — and make the biggest shadow you can, then the smallest, then the strangest.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is around 19 months and shadows are close to magic at this age. Give him the torch on the floor and let him watch his own shadow move on the wall. **He'll try to pick it up.**

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Dark rooms make a familiar house strange, and strange is what a stuck day needs.**
> By late December she has been indoors a great deal and the house has stopped being interesting. Adding another activity doesn't fix that; changing the conditions does — and turning the lights off is the cheapest change available.
> A dark room with one moving light source alters every surface she knows. It also makes ordinary objects perform: a hand becomes a dog, a peg doll becomes a giant. This is the same principle as the den in November and the light table in October — when the day is stuck, change the room rather than the plan. Darkness happens to be the version December supplies for free.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** She made a bird with her hands, on her own, and then had to show every single person in the house.

## 🥱 Bad Weather Day 2: The Nobody Knows What Day It Is Day
**Use when:** It's the stretch between Christmas and New Year, the routine has gone, and everyone is flat and slightly cross.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:20 AM** — Morning Meeting: **Make a Plan for the Day** (20 min)
- **9:20–10:30 AM** — Main Event: The Restaurant (70 min)
- **10:30–10:45 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:45–11:45 AM** — Get Outside: **The Anywhere Walk** (60 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — Free Play (15 min)
- **12:00 PM** — **Lunch, served by her**
- **Afternoon** — **Back to the normal bedtime, starting tonight**

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Restaurant
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A cloth for the table, and any candle or fairy lights
- Card and markers, for menus and an OPEN sign
- A notepad, for orders
- An apron and a tea towel over the arm
- Leftovers, bread, cheese, fruit — whatever's actually in
- Her knife and board from Week 48
- A tray

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Write the day's plan on a sheet of paper first**, together, with times. **Three things on it, that's all.** The plan is the actual remedy today; the restaurant is what fills it.
2. **She writes the menu from what's in the fridge** — Week 48's leftovers challenge, in restaurant costume.
3. **Lay a proper table**, cloth and all, and put the OPEN sign on the door.
4. **She takes orders on the pad**, in her own writing, and repeats them back.
5. **She makes what she can** — spreading, slicing, arranging — and an adult does anything hot.
6. **Serves it with a tea towel over the arm**, and comes back to ask how it was.
7. **Then the bill, in dots or scribbles**, and everybody pays in buttons.

> 💡 **Tip:** Restore the normal bedtime tonight, even though the week is still strange. Most of the crossness in this stretch is accumulated late nights, and it takes about three days to unwind.

### 🌳 Get Outside: The Anywhere Walk
**No destination and no purpose.** She leads and chooses every turn. **Say yes to all of it**, including the boring route and the one that goes in a circle. Forty minutes of cold air is the second half of today's remedy.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is a customer and will eat off her tray with his hands. At 19 months, being served by his sister is the best restaurant experience available to him.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **The week between the holidays is hard because the shape has gone, not because anything is wrong.**
> Small children run on routine far more than adults do, and this particular week removes almost all of it — no normal meals, no normal bedtimes, different people in the house, no ordinary days to hang anything on. The result is a child who is unaccountably difficult and an adult who can't work out why.
> The fix isn't more entertainment; it's the return of a shape. Three things written on a piece of paper in the morning, meals at roughly the usual times, and the normal bedtime back tonight will do more for the mood of the house than any outing. She isn't bored. She's unmoored, which looks the same and needs the opposite treatment.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Nobody in this house knows what day it is and she's the one taking it worst. Bedtime back to normal, starting tonight.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1: Poorly at Christmas
**Use when:** She's genuinely unwell on or near a big day, and something has to be given up.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Nest on the sofa, near the tree or the window
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Slow Present (20–40 min, in bursts)
- **Snack** — Whatever she'll take, and plenty to drink
- **Late morning** — Wind-Down: The Window Bed
- **Nap early**, and let it run

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Slow Present
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- One present, unopened
- Cushions, blankets, and tissues within reach
- A drink with a straw
- A tray, for whatever the present turns out to be
- A phone, to call whoever she's missing
- The audio story from an earlier sick day, or her radio play from Day 341

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Say the honest thing first, plainly and without a fuss:** she's poorly, so today isn't going to be the day everyone planned, **and the day can be moved.** Christmas dinner can happen on the 27th. Nobody minds.
2. **Build the nest somewhere she can see the tree or the window**, not shut away in a bedroom.
3. **One present, opened as slowly as possible.** Look at the paper. Guess the shape. Shake it. **Stretching one present across an hour is exactly right for a child with no energy.**
4. **Then play with it on the tray, in the nest**, for as long as it holds.
5. **Phone or video-call whoever she's missing**, briefly. **Three minutes, not twenty.**
6. **Save the rest of the presents.** A poorly child opening eleven things remembers none of them and enjoys none of them.
7. **Play her own radio play back** in the afternoon — familiar, hers, and requires nothing.

> 💡 **Tip:** Move the day rather than compressing it. A family that eats its Christmas dinner on the 28th because a four-year-old had a temperature has done the right thing, and she will remember that it was moved for her.

### 😴 Wind-Down: The Window Bed
Nest by the window with the lights off inside and whatever's out there — lights on houses, rain, the dark. **Nothing to do, nothing required.** Ill children need lower input than they're usually given on a big day.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** If Kreston's got it too, the day is a write-off and should be declared one out loud, early, so nobody spends it disappointed.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Being ill on a big day is disappointing for the adults, and mostly confusing for her.**
> The grief on a spoiled Christmas is largely the grown-ups' — the plans, the preparation, the picture of how it was supposed to look. A poorly four-year-old isn't measuring the day against an imagined version; she just feels rotten and doesn't understand why everyone seems tense.
> Which means the useful move is to protect her from the disappointment rather than sharing it. Say clearly that the day can be moved, move it, and keep the atmosphere flat and kind. A calm sofa day with one present and a parent nearby is a perfectly good memory. A tense day of everyone trying to salvage the schedule around a sick child is not, and she'll pick up every bit of it.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** We did Christmas dinner two days late one year and it was better. Nobody has ever mentioned the date since.

## 🤧 Sick Day 2: The Between-Days Recovery
**Use when:** She's on the mend, the house is still chaotic, and she needs something small and hers.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Slow start, her pace
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Museum of This Year (30–45 min, in bursts)
- **Snack** — At the table if she'll manage it
- **Late morning** — **The Doorstep Ten Minutes**
- **Wind-Down** — Reading the storybook and the field guide
- **Nap whenever she'll take it**

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Museum of This Year
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A tray or a low shelf
- Objects from the year: the fossils, the crystal jar, the track slabs, the seed packets, the storybook, the field guide, the moon journal
- Card and markers, for the labels
- Sticky tack
- A cushion to sit on

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Everything happens on one tray, sitting down.** Bring the objects to her; she doesn't fetch anything.
2. **Spread them out and let her handle them.** She'll remember things you've forgotten entirely.
3. **Six exhibits, no more.** She chooses which.
4. **Each one gets a label** — she dictates what it is and where it came from, you write it.
5. **Ask her which was the best day of the year.** Write the answer on a card. **Do not react to it**, whatever it is.
6. **Then she gives the tour**, from where she's sitting, to one person at a time.
7. **Leave the tray out** for the rest of the week. It's a good thing to have on the table on Day 365.

> 💡 **Tip:** Her answer to *best day of the year* will not be the one you'd pick, and it's worth writing down verbatim and putting in the capsule tomorrow. It's the most honest review this book will ever get.

### 🌳 Get Outside: The Doorstep Ten Minutes
**Coat on, ten minutes on the step.** Look at the sky, check the ice lantern if there's one left, fill the bird feeder. **Not a walk** — just outside air on a recovering child, which genuinely helps.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston takes the exhibits off the tray and walks away with them. Give him one item that's genuinely his — his handprint fossil from Day 303 — and let him carry that around instead.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **What she remembers of a year is not what you remember, and hers is the more useful list.**
> Adults recall the events: the trip, the party, the big day, the things that took planning. Children of this age reliably nominate something small and specific — a puddle, a morning, a thing that broke, a joke somebody made — and it almost never matches the effort spent.
> That's worth hearing without correcting or steering. It's real information about what actually lands, and it's the best possible argument for the ordinary days in this book. If she names the day the water run flooded the patio over the day with the cake and the guests, that isn't her getting it wrong. That's the answer to the question the whole year was asking.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Ask her. Write down exactly what she says. Don't argue with it.

## ✨ December — Extra Ideas
### *When you just need one more thing*

---

### 🎲 Alternative Activities

**Check the Crystal Jars** — Thirty seconds a day, one mark on the chart. They keep growing for weeks.

**Fill the Bird Feeders** — Every morning. It's a promise now.

**The Twenty-Minute Bird Count** — Clipboard, timer, window. Once a week all winter.

**Shadow Puppets** — Torch, wall, hands. No preparation whatsoever.

**The Village** — Week 49's cardboard street is still there. New residents, new problems.

**Read Her Own Book** — The storybook from Day 340, out loud, to anyone visiting.

**Sink or Float in the Bath** — Every bath from now on. Two objects and a prediction.

**Foil Boats** — One square, one bowl, count the coins. Five minutes, endlessly repeatable.

**Torch Walk at Four O'Clock** — It's dark. Use it rather than resenting it.

**The Ice Bucket Overnight** — Anything left in water outside comes back changed. Check it in the morning.

**Kreston's Turn** — He's 18–19 months. A bowl of water and cups on a towel, a torch in a dark room, and a box to climb in and out of. All three will run for twenty minutes each.

---

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts

**Paper Snowflakes** — Folded into sixths, not fourths. Six is the rule.

**Ice Lanterns** — A balloon of water frozen overnight, a battery light inside.

**Salt Dough Decorations** — Cut, dried, painted, threaded. Keep them for next year.

**Shadow Puppet Cutting** — Black card and lolly sticks. Any character from any story.

**Handprint Reindeer** — Brown handprint, red thumbprint nose. Dated, in the keepsake box.

**Wrapping Paper Printing** — Potato stamps or leaf prints on brown paper. Better than shop-bought.

**Thank-You Cards** — Drawn front, dictated inside, her signature.

**The 2028 Calendar** — Twelve boxes, seasons coloured in, birthdays marked.

**Frozen Bubbles** — If it drops below about -7°C, blow bubbles outside and watch them frost over. Rare and worth chasing.

**Pinecone Bird Feeders** — Lard, seed, string. Messy and immediately useful.

**Star Cutting** — Five-pointed, from one folded square, in one cut. Good for the top of anything.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **The book ends but the things it started don't.**
> There are bulbs in a pot, seeds in envelopes, a jar with something rotting in it, a log pile, a tree with a file, bird feeders that need filling, and a sealed box that gets opened next December. None of those are finished, and all of them belong to next year.
> That's deliberate, and it's the honest way for a year of days to end. The value was never in the activities — it was in the habit of going outside, looking closely, asking what something is for, and coming back to check. Those don't stop on the 31st of December. If the whole book gets forgotten and the only things that survive are the tree visits and the bird feeder, that's not a failure of the book. That's the book having worked.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Everything in this booklet that isn't finished was left unfinished on purpose. Next year does the rest.
