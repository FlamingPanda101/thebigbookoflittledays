# 🌧️ January — Backup Days & Extra Ideas

These five pages sit outside the 365. Reach for them when the day you had
planned will not run: ice on the sidewalk and a stroller that will not go
over it, a fever that arrived at 3:00 in the morning, or an hour where the
plan in front of you needs more hands than you have. The two weather days
are full days built for a January where nobody is leaving the house, so
everything on them comes out of your own kitchen. The two sick days run on
no clock at all.

---

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 1 — Cold Enough to Keep

Freezing rain since before it got light, the ground hard under it, and dark
again by five. The door stays shut today. The cold is still the subject, and
it comes in through the kitchen instead of the front step.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Put the fruit bowl, the salt and a ball of string on the counter, stand the vinegar and a clean jar with a lid beside them, and clear the sill above the warmest radiator.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: What Lives in the Cold
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: Drying the Apple Rings (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Twenty Seconds of Weather (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: The Tray of Things That Keep (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🎬 Movie Hour: The One She Picks (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: Checking the Apple String
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Tray She Ranked
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Bath and Three Books
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: What Lives in the Cold

**🧰 You need:** the fridge · the cupboard · 2 dish towels laid on the floor.

1. Let her take ten things out of the fridge and ten out of the cupboard, one at a time, and stand each one on its own towel.
2. Swap two over while she watches. Ketchup on the cupboard towel, dried pasta on the fridge towel. Wait for her to object.
3. Ask what would happen to the milk if it lived in the cupboard. Take her answer as it comes, tell her you will show her at 9:15, and put it all back.

### 🎨 The Main Event: Drying the Apple Rings

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 3 apples, or any firm fruit in the bowl; with no fruit in the house, 1 potato or 2 thick slices of bread
- 1 sharp knife (**adult only**)
- String, about a yard, and 2 clothespins
- 1 saucer and 1 marker

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Slice the fruit across into rings about as thick as her fingernail. You cut, she counts them into a bowl and tells you when to stop.
2. Set two rings aside on a saucer. That saucer gets nothing done to it, and it does more work today than anything else on the table.
3. Thread the rest of the rings onto the string with a gap between each one, hang it across the sill above the warm radiator, high up and well past her reach, and pin each end with a clothespin so it cannot slide.
4. Write the date on the saucer, and look at it together every time either of you walks past it.
5. Check the apple string at 4:00 as her job. Almost nothing will have changed, and the almost-nothing is the honest answer to how long this takes.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not skip the saucer that gets no treatment. The ring on the string means nothing to her without a brown one sitting beside it to compare against.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Twenty Seconds of Weather

Coats, hats and boots on as though the walk were happening, because the
getting ready was half of what the walk did for her. Open the back door,
stand on the mat together, and count to twenty out loud while the cold works
on your faces. Shut it. Then run five laps of the downstairs to get warm
again and count those too. Three rounds of that. She gets the weather on her
skin, the door stays shut the rest of the day, and the running gives her back
what the walk would have.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 6 to 7 months, sitting with his hands
> free and rocking on hands and knees. Fill a small plastic bottle with cold
> water, tape the cap down hard, and let him hold it for a minute at a time
> while you slice. Cold in the hands is new information for him. Bring him to
> the open door with a hat on for five of the twenty seconds, facing out, and
> then get him back in the warm. The apple string and the knife stay above
> head height the whole morning.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Tray of Things That Keep

The morning hung fruit up to see what warm air takes out of it. This
afternoon she eats her way across a cupboard full of food other people
already made last, then packs a jar of her own and waits a day for it.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 large plate or tray
- Six of these, or any six the cupboard holds: raisins, dried apricots, olives, pickles, jam, honey, canned peaches, dried beef
- 1 teaspoon each
- 1 clean jar with a lid
- 1 carrot, half a cucumber or any firm vegetable in the drawer
- White vinegar, 1/2 cup · water, 1/2 cup · sugar, 1 tablespoon · salt, 1 teaspoon
- 1 marker and a strip of paper for the label

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Set the six foods around the tray with a spoon each. She tastes them in whatever order she picks, and she is allowed to spit one out.
2. Ask her to lay them in a line from the one she would eat again to the one she would not. Write the order down and read it back to her.
3. Give her the one fact under all six: every one of them was made to last past the week it was picked in. Then let her have her favorite twice.
4. Peel the vegetable into sticks together. She snaps them to the length she wants and packs them into the jar herself.
5. Measure the vinegar, water, sugar and salt into a cup. Hand her the lid and let her shake it until the sugar has gone.
6. Pour it over the sticks, screw the lid down tight, and let her write the date on the label however her writing comes out.
7. Put the jar in the fridge and tell her it is tomorrow's snack. The waiting is the part she will remember.

> 💡 **Tip:** She will hate at least one of the six and say so at volume. Write that one down with the same care as the ones she liked. The ranking belongs to her, and it is the reason she keeps tasting.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Skip the tasting. Pack the jar and stop there.
- Dry whatever else is in the fruit bowl on the sill and hang it in the window.
- Line up everything in the fridge door from what lasts longest to what spoils first.
- Read the dates on six packets and find the one that runs out soonest.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Sit him at the table end in his chair where he
> can watch the jar being shaken. At 6 to 7 months the sound of something
> rattling in a container holds him longer than the container does. Give him a
> clean metal spoon and a small unbreakable cup of his own to bang and mouth.
> Nothing off the tray reaches him: honey waits until he is one, raisins and
> olives are choking hazards at his size, and the vinegar gets a hard no.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Preservation is a surprisingly big idea.**
> Food goes bad because something alive is eating it before you get to it.
> Cold slows those things down, salt pulls the water out from under them,
> vinegar sours the ground they grow in, and drying takes away the water they
> need. Azlyn will not hold all four of those today, and she does not have to.
> She holds one comparison: the ring on the saucer went brown and soft, the
> ring on the string went hard and pale, and both of them came off the same
> fruit an hour apart. Running two versions of a thing and looking at the
> difference is the method behind most of what she will learn later, and she
> can do it at three.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** The knife is adult-only and goes back out of reach between
> cuts, on a high shelf and not the counter edge. Hang the apple string above
> her head and take it down the moment the rings are dry. A loop of string at
> toddler height is a strangulation risk. Radiators get hot enough to burn, so
> the rings sit on the sill above and never on the metal. No honey for Kreston
> before his first birthday, and no raisins, olives or carrot sticks.

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 2 — What Everything Is Made Of

Snow came down in the night and froze under a skin of ice, so every sidewalk
is a slide and the light goes again by five. A whole day indoors makes the
house feel smaller by about eleven in the morning. Today she goes through it
room by room and finds out that the house she knows is made of four or five
things.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Stand an empty laundry basket by the sofa with a roll of masking tape and a marker in it. Dig out the coin jar, the dullest copper you own, and the ketchup.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: The Back of Her Hand
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Coldest Thing in the House (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Sixty-Second Snowstorm (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: The Coins That Come Back (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🛁 The Afternoon Bath (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: The Forks Back in the Drawer
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Basket by the Sofa
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Two Books Under the Lamp
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: The Back of Her Hand

**🧰 You need:** 1 metal spoon · 1 wooden spoon · 1 dish towel · a clear counter.

1. Lay all three side by side and leave them for five minutes so they come to the same temperature as the room.
2. Show her the test: back of the hand, held flat, counting to three. Cheek for the ones that pass.
3. Ask her which is coldest. She picks the metal one, and the three have been sitting together the whole time. Tell her you are going hunting for the coldest thing in the house at 9:15.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Coldest Thing in the House

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 laundry basket
- 4 dish towels laid out on the rug as the four groups
- Masking tape, cut into labels, and 1 marker
- Paper and a pencil for the list
- 1 metal spoon, carried as the thing everything gets compared against

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Walk the house room by room with the basket. She tests with the back of her hand and takes anything that feels cold. Her wrong ones come too.
2. Tip the basket out on the rug and line the finds up, coldest at one end. She sets the order and she can retest as often as she wants.
3. Read the line back to her from cold to warm, and write it down in that order.
4. Now the second sort. Write **metal**, **wood**, **plastic** and **cloth** on four strips of masking tape, stick one to each towel, and read them to her. Every object lands on a towel.
5. She will put things in the wrong group, and the painted ones will be the ones that beat her. Let her tap them, weigh them in her hand and scratch a corner with a fingernail before she commits.
6. Stand back and look at the two arrangements together. Most of what she called cold is sitting on the metal towel. Say that out loud once and let it sit.

> 💡 **Tip:** The whole house is one temperature this morning, and that is what makes this worth an hour. Metal pulls the heat out of her hand faster than wood does, so it feels colder while measuring the same. She does not need those words today. She needs to notice that the spoon and the cutting board disagree.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: The Sixty-Second Snowstorm

Tip the paper recycling onto the floor and crumple the lot into snowballs
with her. Fifty is a good target and she will lose interest at thirty, so
crumple the rest yourself. Run a line of masking tape across the middle of
the room, put her on one side and you on the other, and set a timer for sixty
seconds. One rule: get the snow off your own side. Count what is left when
the timer goes, sweep it back to the middle and run it again. Three rounds
takes more out of her than the walk would have, and the cleanup is one bag.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 6 to 7 months and mouths everything he
> can reach. Sit him propped against the sofa with this morning's wooden spoon
> and metal spoon and watch which one he keeps. Cold metal on his gums is a
> sensation he has not had before. Keep him out of the room while the paper is
> flying. Wet paper tears into pieces the size of his mouth, so sweep the floor
> at his eye level before he goes back down, and give him one clean sheet to
> crumple at the edge of the room with you sitting beside him.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Coins That Come Back

The morning was full of cold things she was told to leave as they were. This
afternoon she takes the dullest metal in the house and makes it shine, and
the change happens under her own hands inside a minute.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 20 or so dull coins, copper ones for preference
- Ketchup, 2 tablespoons, or 1 tablespoon of salt stirred into 3 tablespoons of vinegar
- 1 small bowl and 1 old teaspoon
- 2 rags or a cut-up t-shirt
- 1 bowl of clean water and a dish towel
- The forks and spoons out of the drawer

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Line the coins up on a rag and let her pick the three ugliest ones.
2. Stand one of those three aside where she can see it. Nothing happens to that coin all afternoon, and it is the one that proves the rest.
3. Drop the others into the bowl of ketchup and let her push them under with the spoon. Count to sixty out loud together.
4. Fish one out and rub it with the rag. It comes up bright. Hand her the rag and let her do every remaining coin herself.
5. Rinse them all in the clean water and dry them, because ketchup left sitting on metal marks it.
6. Line the bright coins up next to the one you left alone and ask her to tell you what happened. Write her explanation down word for word.
7. Finish on the silverware. A dry rag and ten minutes of buffing the forks, and she leaves them ready on the counter for 4:00.

> 💡 **Tip:** Sixty seconds is a long minute for a three-year-old. Count it out loud with her, or she fishes them out at twenty and the trick fails on her.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Coins in ketchup and nothing else. Ten minutes, same result.
- Polish one object each and stand them in a row on the windowsill.
- Sort the coin jar by size, then by how bright each one is.
- Wash the plastic toys in a bowl of warm soapy water on a towel on the floor.

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Coins stay on the table and off the floor the
> whole afternoon. Count them into the bowl and count them back into the jar
> before he comes down. Give him the wooden spoon and an unbreakable cup on
> the rug where you can see him. He is rocking on hands and knees and getting
> further with it every week, so what sat out of his reach on Monday is inside
> it by Friday. Get down at his eye level and check the floor before you put
> him on it.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Sorting by material is harder than sorting by color.**
> Color sits on the surface and she can call it from across the room. Material
> hides. A wooden spoon and a plastic spoon come in the same shape and often
> the same color, and telling them apart means picking both up, feeling the
> weight, tapping them and holding them against her cheek. She has to gather
> evidence and hold a rule in her head while she gathers it, which is a bigger
> job than matching what she sees. Expect wrong groups, and expect painted
> wood and coated metal to break her rule. The arguing over the hard ones is
> the part that teaches, so let those arguments run long.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** The temperature test is for cold things. Say the rule out
> loud before she starts and again after lunch: she tests nothing on the
> stove, nothing on a radiator, nothing plugged in, and she asks you first
> every single time. Coins are one of the most swallowed objects there is, so
> count them out and count them back before Kreston is on the floor. Keep the
> vinegar and the bowl at the back of the counter, and wash her hands before
> the snack.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1 — The Day She Stays on the Sofa

This is the flat day. She is hot, she wants you inside arm's reach, and
anything with a beginning and an end is past her. Everything on this page can
be picked up and dropped without a word, and none of it needs cutting,
setting up or clearing away.

### 🕗 The Shape of the Day

- **Whenever she wakes** · 🛋️ Making the Spot, and no decisions asked of her
- **Once she is in it** · the first drink, small and often
- **When lying still stops working** · 🫱 Lap Things: one at a time, dropped the moment it stops holding
- **After the first long sleep** · more Lap Things, or the movie, with no guilt attached to either
- **The middle of the day** · 😴 The Rest Block
- **When she asks for food** · small, bland, and only when she asks
- **Before the afternoon dip** · a warm bath if she has it in her, a warm washcloth if she does not
- **The end of the day** · bed an hour early and the same book twice

### 🛋️ Making the Spot

Build her one place and let her stay in it. Sofa, her pillow from her own
bed, the blanket she picks, a towel underneath in case of the bowl, and a
lidded cup of water within her reach and not yours. Put a bucket or a mixing
bowl on the floor beside her and show her where it is, so she does not have
to call for it. Move a lamp so the overhead light can stay off.

Set the day up so she never has to move for anything. Fetching is your job
today, and the fetching is most of what she wants from you.

### 😴 The Rest Block

Sleep is the medicine here, and the rest block is the plan rather than the
gap between plans. Half shut the curtains, drop the sound in the house, and
lie on the floor beside the sofa where she can see you. Skip the screen for
the first stretch, because she will fight sleep to keep watching. An hour is
a good aim and forty minutes is a win. If Kreston goes down at the same time,
put your own head on the arm of the sofa and take the twenty minutes. You are
on the third night of broken sleep too, and nobody else in the house is going
to schedule that for you.

### 🫱 Lap Things

- **The Window List** · she names everything that goes past and you write it on the back of an envelope
- **The Story With Her Name In It** · you make it up as you go, and she chooses what happens next when she has it in her
- **The Warm Washcloth** · a bowl of warm water on the floor and a cloth she wipes her own face and hands with
- **Five Minutes on One Page** · one page of a picture book held open that long, hunting for small things in the corners
- **The Popsicle That Counts as a Drink** · for a raw throat, and it goes on the fluids tally
- **Every Photo Named Out Loud** · your phone on airplane mode and the camera roll gone through one picture at a time

### 🍵 When She Asks for Food

Fluids matter today and food does not. Water, diluted juice, milk if it stays
down, popsicles, ice chips, soup broth from a mug. Offer small amounts often
rather than a meal she has to face down. Toast, banana, plain rice, dry
cereal by the handful. She eats again when she is better, and pushing a plate
at her today costs you both more than the missed meal does.

### 👶 Where Kreston Goes

He is 6 to 7 months, sitting on his own and rocking on hands and knees, and
every week that rocking gets him a hand's width further, so anything of hers
left within reach of his blanket is his. Set his blanket and a basket of his
own things on the far side of the room where he can see her and not reach her,
and take the playpen or travel crib out if you have one, since today is the
day it earns its space. Wash your hands between them and keep her cup, her
spoon and her washcloth off his patch. The odds are he catches it anyway, and
the handwashing still buys you a couple of days. Feed him and change him on a
schedule you set out loud, because she cannot wait for anything today and he
will get skipped otherwise.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A day off has to be visibly protected or it doesn't happen.**
> A sick day is a day off nobody chose, and it disappears the same way a
> chosen one does. The delivery still comes, the message still gets answered
> on your knees by the sofa, and the thing you meant to do at four gets tried
> at four. Clear it out loud instead. Cancel the visit in front of her, take
> the appointment off the calendar where she can see the line through it, and
> tell her the day has nothing in it now. She hears that you moved the world
> for her, which is most of what she wants while she feels this bad, and you
> stop spending the day in two places at once.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Keep the fluids going and count wet diapers or trips to the
> bathroom, because dehydration moves faster in a three-year-old than in you.
> Call the doctor for fast or labored breathing, a fever over 102°F that will
> not come down or lasts past three days, a child too drowsy to rouse, no wet
> diaper in eight hours, or any rash that does not fade when you press a glass
> against it. Never give aspirin to a child. Check the label and the weight
> before every dose, and write down the time you gave it.

## 🤒 Sick Day 2 — Better But Not Well

The fever went in the night and she woke up asking for things. She has about
an hour of energy in her with nothing behind it, so the trap today is a plan
built for a well child. Give her a small version of a normal day and let her
come back to full size on her own schedule.

### 🕗 The Shape of the Day

- **Whenever she wakes** · 🛋️ Moving the Spot, which is the whole event
- **The first good hour** · 🫱 Things She Can Do From Sitting, one at a time
- **When she gets sharp with you** · the energy has run out. Back to the blanket
- **After the first long sleep** · food, and more of it than yesterday
- **The middle of the day** · 😴 The Rest Block, and she will fight it
- **When she asks for food** · say yes to almost all of it
- **Before the afternoon dip** · one job with a visible end
- **The end of the day** · an early bath and no plans made out loud for tomorrow

### 🛋️ Moving the Spot

Rebuild her camp in a different room. Kitchen table with a blanket over her
knees, the floor by the window, your bed with the good pillows, anywhere that
is not yesterday's sofa. A change of room does more for a bored recovering
child than any activity you could put in front of her, and it costs you four
minutes and an armful of bedding.

Take the whole kit with her: cup, blanket, bowl within reach, the same lamp.
Then move it again after lunch if the second room stops working.

### 😴 The Rest Block

She will refuse it today, and she still needs it. Take sleep off the table
and ask for lying down instead. Curtains half shut, a story playing, and
nothing she has to answer. Forty minutes flat on her back does most of what a
nap would have, and about half the time she goes under anyway. Sit with her
for the first five minutes, then get up and go do the thing you have not
managed for two days.

### 🫱 Things She Can Do From Sitting

- **The Drawing She Doesn't Have to Explain** · a hardback book under the paper for a desk, crayons loose on the blanket, and no questions about what it is
- **The Button Tin on a Tray** · sort them however she likes, tip them out, do it again
- **The Order Pad** · she writes an order from the blanket however her writing comes out and you fetch it, one item at a time
- **Rolling It Back** · she rolls a ball across the floor to you from where she sits and you roll it back, for as long as it holds her
- **Reading to Kreston** · board books held up from the sofa while he sits below her on his blanket
- **Joseph on Speaker** · him or a grandparent, and she does the talking with the phone flat on her chest
- **One Sticker Sheet, One Page** · no rules, no theme, and the sheet is hers alone

### 🍽️ When She Asks for Food

Her appetite comes back before her energy does, and it comes back crooked.
Say yes to toast at four in the afternoon and soup for breakfast. Keep
offering fluids at the same rate as yesterday even though she looks better,
because she stops asking before she has caught up. Put a small plate in front
of her rather than a full one, and let her ask for seconds. Asking for
seconds is a better sign than a plate she clears without noticing.

### 👶 Where Kreston Goes

Today she wants him near, and near is now the problem. He is 6 to 7 months,
he mouths everything he reaches, and half of what she is playing with fits in
his mouth. Put him on his blanket below her rather than beside her, run the
button tin on a tray at sofa height, and count the buttons back in before he
gets floor time. Give her one job that involves him and ends: holding the
book up for three pages, passing him one toy at a time, singing him the song
he likes. She gets to be the older one again for ten minutes, which is worth
more to her right now than anything you could hand her on a tray.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **"What is it?" is the question to stop asking.**
> She finishes something on the blanket and hands it over. The reflex is to
> ask what it is, and she will invent an answer to satisfy you, which turns
> her drawing into a test with one right answer she has to guess. Say what you
> see instead. You used the blue three times. That line runs right off the
> edge. This bit here is pressed so hard it went through the paper. She will
> tell you what it is inside a minute, or she will tell you it is nothing at
> all, and both of those are fine. At three the movement and the mark are the
> point, and the naming arrives later on its own.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Buttons, coins and small sorting objects stay on a tray at
> sofa height and get counted back into the tin before Kreston is on the
> floor. A button is the right size to block a 7-month-old's airway. She can
> be around other children once she has gone 24 hours with no fever and no
> medicine holding it down, and not before. Keep giving fluids at yesterday's
> rate, and call the doctor if a fever comes back after a clear day.

## 🎲 January — Extra Ideas

Ten minutes, no plan, and a dark afternoon to fill. Nothing here needs a
shopping trip or more than one instruction.

### 🎲 Alternative Activities

- **The Dark by Five Countdown** · she checks the window every fifteen minutes and shouts when the streetlights come on
- **The Hallway Tow** · she kneels on a bath towel holding the front edge and you pull her from the bedroom door to the kitchen and back
- **Every Shoe in the House** · lined up biggest to smallest along the hall with yours at the end
- **The Two-Minute Race** · a timer, one room, and everything that lives somewhere else goes in a basket
- **The Upside-Down Meal** · dinner food at breakfast time, eaten on a blanket on the floor
- **The Teddy Toboggan** · send soft toys down the stairs on a cushion and mark whose goes furthest
- **Three Mugs, Three Temperatures** · cool, warm and warmer, and she picks the one that is right
- **The Fogged Mirror** · run the hot water, let the mirror steam up and draw in it with a finger
- **Ten Things You Can Hear From the Sofa** · sit still, eyes shut, and count them off on her fingers
- **The Reverse Tidy** · you put things in the wrong rooms and she finds all six
- **The Boot Check** · every pair of boots by the door tried on by the wrong person

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts

- **The Frost Picture** · white crayon on white paper, then a wash of watery blue over the top
- **The Salt Bloom** · sprinkle table salt into a wet blue wash and watch the blotches open while it dries
- **The Cotton Swab Snow** · white paint dots on the darkest paper in the house
