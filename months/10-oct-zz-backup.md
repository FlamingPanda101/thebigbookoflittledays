# 🌧️ October Backup Days
## *For when the plan doesn't survive the morning*

**October's version of a bad day:** the first real storm, and then the clocks change and it's suddenly dark before teatime — which shortens the usable day by two hours and takes about a fortnight to adjust to. Add the colds that go round when the weather turns, and a week of three holidays at the end of the month, and October will need these more than most.

---

<a id="oct-weather-1"></a>

## ⛈️ Bad Weather Day 1: The Storm Day
**Use when:** It's genuinely wild out — wind, rain, and no version of outside that's worth attempting.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting: Listen to It (15 min)
- **9:15–10:30 AM** — Main Event: The Bowling Alley (75 min)
- **10:30–10:45 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:45–11:30 AM** — Indoor Movement: The Sock Skate (45 min)
- **11:30 AM–12:00 PM** — Quiet Play (30 min)
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch & Nap Prep

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Bowling Alley
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- Ten empty plastic bottles, or cardboard tubes, or tins
- A ball — a football, a tennis ball, or a rolled pair of socks
- Masking tape, for the lane and the throw line
- Paper and a marker, for the scoreboard
- Rice or a little water, to weight the bottles
- Stickers, to number the pins

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Set up the lane down the longest corridor.** Tape the edges and a throw line, and tape a cross where each pin stands so resetting is quick.
2. **Number the pins 1 to 10** with stickers. She writes the numbers if she can.
3. **Weight each bottle with a centimetre of rice** — unweighted bottles fall over if you look at them and the game is over in a minute.
4. **Then the scoreboard, which is the real activity.** Two columns, her name and yours. **After each roll she counts the pins knocked down and marks that many tallies.**
5. **Count the pins by touching each one.** Same rule as Day 276 — one touch, one number.
6. **Five rolls each, then count the tallies in fives.** Who won? By how many?
7. **Then change the game:** roll from further back, roll with the wrong hand, roll backwards through your legs, roll while she calls out which numbered pin to aim for.

> 💡 **Tip:** Let her be the scorekeeper for both players. Holding the pencil turns her from a competitor into an official, which cuts the emotional stakes of losing roughly in half.

### 🏃 Indoor Movement: The Sock Skate
Thick socks on a smooth floor. Skate, glide, race, and stop dead on a signal. Then tow her along the corridor on a towel — heavy work, and it burns a surprising amount of adult energy too.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is around 17 months and this is his game entirely. Set up three pins at his end and let him knock them over with his hands, which is the correct method at his age.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Scorekeeping turns a chaotic game into a calm one.**
> An indoor game with no structure tends to escalate — faster, louder, wilder — until someone gets hurt or upset. A scoreboard interrupts that automatically, because every turn ends with a pause to count and write, and the pauses are what stop the spiral.
> It also smuggles in a lot of maths on a day when nothing was planned. Counting the knocked-down pins, tallying, comparing two totals, working out who's ahead. But the regulation is the real reason to do it: a game with a shape is one a wound-up four-year-old can stay inside for an hour, and a game without one lasts eleven minutes and ends badly.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** She kept score for both of us and I did not win a single round. Draw your own conclusions.

## 🌑 Bad Weather Day 2: The Dark Afternoon
**Use when:** The clocks have changed, it's dark by half four, and the day has lost its second half.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:00 AM** — **Outside first, whatever it's like** — the light is the scarce thing now (45 min)
- **10:00–10:15 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:15–11:30 AM** — Main Event: The Light Table (75 min)
- **11:30 AM–12:00 PM** — Quiet Play (30 min)
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch & Nap Prep
- **Late afternoon** — **Torches on, curtains open**

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Light Table
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A clear plastic storage box, upside down, or a glass baking dish
- A torch, or a string of fairy lights, underneath
- Coloured cellophane, tissue paper, and clear plastic lids
- Coloured jelly sweets, or ice cubes made with food colouring
- A shallow dish of water
- Beads, buttons, and anything translucent
- A dark room, or a sheet over a table

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Torch or fairy lights underneath, box on top, lights off.** The moment the room goes dark and the box glows is the whole reason for the activity.
2. **Start with the cellophane.** One colour, then two overlapping. **Red on blue makes purple** — the same discovery as Day 262, arriving in a different medium.
3. **Sort by whether the light gets through.** Three piles: light goes through, light half goes through, no light at all. **This is a proper sort with a rule she can test.**
4. **Then the water dish on the glass** — drop food colouring in and watch it spread, lit from below.
5. **Coloured ice cubes on the light table** melt into each other and mix, slowly, for half an hour. Set that going early.
6. **Make a picture** — arrange the translucent pieces into a face or a landscape and photograph it lit up before it gets swept away.
7. **Leave it running through the dark afternoon.** It's the best thing in the house at four o'clock in November.

> ⚠️ **Fairy lights and a shallow dish of water need keeping apart.** Battery lights only, and the water dish sits on the glass, not near the battery pack.

> 💡 **Tip:** Get outside in the morning on days like this, even briefly and even in poor weather. Once the clocks change, the daylight has to be spent when it exists rather than saved for later.

### 🏃 Indoor Movement: Torch Tag
Lights off, two torches, and everyone has to keep out of the other beam. Then torch hide-and-seek. Then everyone lies on the floor and makes shapes on the ceiling.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston gets his own torch and the light table at floor level with only large, safe, unswallowable pieces. **No jelly sweets and no small beads** in his version.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **The clocks changing costs her more than it costs you.**
> An hour's shift takes an adult a couple of days. For a small child it can take a fortnight, and it arrives as early waking, a wrecked bedtime, and a fortnight of being unaccountably difficult at five in the afternoon.
> Knowing that in advance changes how you read the behaviour. It also suggests the two things that actually help: getting outside in daylight in the morning, which is the strongest signal the body clock has, and shifting bedtime by ten minutes at a time rather than all at once. And it means late October's difficult fortnight isn't a regression or something you've done wrong — it's a clock, resetting slowly.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Awake at five, wrecked by four. It's the clocks, it lasts about two weeks, and then she'll be fine.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1: The Comic
**Use when:** She's properly under the weather — flat, hot, and not up to using her hands for much.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Build the nest, no rush (20 min)
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Comic (20–40 min, in bursts)
- **Snack** — Warm, and plenty to drink
- **Late morning** — Wind-Down: Read it back, then read to her
- **Nap early**, and let it run

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Comic
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A few sheets of paper, ruled into six boxes each — **you do the ruling**
- Pencils and a black pen, for you
- Crayons, within her reach
- A clipboard or a book, to lean on
- A drink with a straw
- Cushions and blankets

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Rule the boxes yourself before you start.** Six per page, big and simple.
2. **She's the writer and you're the artist.** That's the deal, and it's the whole point on a day when her hands are heavy.
3. **Ask what happens in box one.** Draw it, badly, while she watches. **Being visibly bad at it is useful** — she'll start correcting you, which is participation without effort.
4. **Write her words underneath each box, exactly as she says them**, speech bubbles and all.
5. **Six boxes is a whole story** and it's achievable in twenty minutes with breaks. If she stalls after three, three is a fine comic.
6. **She colours in whichever bits she wants**, or none, from lying down.
7. **Read it back to her at the end, out loud, with voices.** Then again, because she'll ask.

> 💡 **Tip:** Put her, you, and Kreston in it by name. A story about people she knows holds a poorly four-year-old's attention far better than one about invented characters.

### 😴 Wind-Down: Read It Back
Read her comic, then read her something familiar, in a dark room, with no expectation of participation. If she drifts off mid-way, that's the day working as intended.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** If Kreston's got it too, put both of them in the same nest and lower every standard in the house. Draw him into the comic; he can't follow it and he'll like hearing his name.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Illness is a good time to be the one with the ideas rather than the hands.**
> Most of what she does needs her body — cutting, building, running, climbing. On a bad day none of that is available, and the usual response is a screen, because everything else seems to require more than she's got.
> But the imagining is unimpaired. She can invent a whole story while lying flat, if someone else does the drawing and the writing. Splitting the job that way — her ideas, your hands — keeps her doing something genuinely creative on a day when she can't hold a pencil for long. It's also a nice reversal: she's directing an adult, which is a rare and cheering position to be in when you feel terrible.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** In her comic, Kreston eats the moon and I have to go and get it back. It's the best thing anyone in this house has written.

## 🤧 Sick Day 2: The Sorting Tray
**Use when:** She's on the mend — bored, irritable, wants something to do but can't sustain anything.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Slow start, no demands (30 min)
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Sorting Tray (20–40 min, in bursts)
- **Snack** — Whatever she'll take
- **Late morning** — Wind-Down: The Audio Story
- **Nap whenever she'll have it**

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Sorting Tray
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A tray with a rim, or a baking tin
- A box of mixed small things — buttons, bottle tops, beads, coins, shells, pasta, lids
- Several small pots, muffin tins, or egg boxes
- Tweezers or a teaspoon
- Paper and a marker, for labels

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Set the tray on her lap or on a low table beside the nest.** Everything within reach, nothing requiring her to get up.
2. **Tip it out and let her look before suggesting anything.** She may well start sorting without being asked.
3. **First sort: her rule.** Whatever she picks — colour, size, favourite. **Don't offer a category.**
4. **When that's done, ask for a different rule** and re-sort the same objects. **Re-sorting the same set by a new rule is the harder task** and it's the one worth getting to.
5. Tweezers for the small ones if she wants the challenge, fingers if she doesn't.
6. **Label the pots** with her drawings once she's settled on the groups.
7. **Tip it all back in the box at the end**, which she'll want to do herself so she can start again tomorrow.

> ⚠️ **Buttons, beads and coins are choking-sized.** This is a tray-on-a-lap activity while Kreston is elsewhere, and everything goes back in the box and up on a shelf when she's finished.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't ask her to explain her rule while she's sorting. Ask afterwards, once the pots are done — being interrogated mid-task is what ends it.

### 😴 Wind-Down: The Audio Story
An audio story with the lights low. **Recovery days are the right time for a longer one than usual** — she's stationary anyway, and a story with no pictures does more listening work than any screen would.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston gets a completely separate tray with three large objects and a pot, well away from hers, or he does this at a different time. **No shared trays on a small-parts day.**

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A job with a visible end is what a recovering child wants.**
> On the mend she has just enough energy to be frustrated and not enough to start anything open-ended. Open-ended play, which is usually the good stuff, is exactly wrong on this day — it requires her to generate the direction, and generating is the thing she hasn't got.
> A tray of mixed objects and some empty pots is the opposite: the task is obvious, the end is visible, and finishing it is achievable from a horizontal position. That's the shape to reach for on every recovery morning — small, contained, clearly finished. The open-ended stuff comes back the day she starts complaining about being bored properly.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Sorted by colour, then by size, then by "ones I like". The third category had one button in it.

## ✨ October — Extra Ideas
### *When you just need one more thing*

---

### 🎲 Alternative Activities

**The Leaf Pile, Again** — It's the best month for it and it costs nothing. Rake, jump, repeat.

**Conker Championship** — Adult drills, she threads, everyone plays. Traditional and short.

**The Number Line** — The tape from Day 274 is still on the floor. Dice, jumps, five minutes at a time.

**The Moon Check** — Even after the journal's finished, look for it every clear night. She'll keep noticing the shape.

**Pumpkin Seeds, Roasted** — Any pumpkin, any week. Rinse, oil, salt, 15 minutes at 180°C.

**The Ramp, Again** — Baking tray and a stack of books. Ten minutes of set-up, an hour of use.

**Puddle Depths** — Wellies, a stick, and a hunt for the deepest one on the street.

**The Box Car** — Still in the hall from Day 282. Give it a new destination and it's a new game.

**Torch Walk** — Dark by five now. Twenty minutes with two torches beats an hour indoors.

**Web Spotting** — Spray bottle, early morning, along a hedge. Best in autumn.

**Kreston's Turn** — He's 16–17 months. A tray of dry leaves to crunch, a container with a hole to post things into, and a low box to climb on and off. He'll cycle through all three for an hour.

---

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts

**Leaf Printing** — Paint the underside, press, roll, peel. Never stops working.

**Pumpkin Face Drawing** — Marker straight onto the skin, wiped off and redrawn as many times as she likes.

**Salt Dough Fossils** — Any shell, leaf, or hand. Cheap, and they keep.

**Foil Planets** — Scrunched foil balls in different sizes, hung on string from a coat hanger.

**Pierced Card Constellations** — Adult pierces, she holds the torch. Ten minutes, big effect.

**Paper Bug Camouflage** — Cut, colour, hide. The whole activity from Day 299 in twenty minutes.

**Spider Web Weaving** — Wool taped across a corner or a paper plate with holes punched round the edge.

**Papel Picado** — Folded tissue, snipped and opened out. Good for any occasion, not only November.

**Rangoli Chalk Patterns** — Circle, petals, symmetry. On the doorstep, washed off by the rain.

**Handprint Bats and Spiders** — Black paint, folded handprints, googly eyes. Fast and effective.

**The Giant Model, Again** — Any creature, boxes and newspaper, built to a specification card.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Dark afternoons change the shape of the day, and the plan has to change with them.**
> From the end of October, the second half of the afternoon stops being usable in the way it was. The stretch between four and bedtime is the hardest part of the day in winter — everyone's tired, it's dark, and the obvious solution is a screen at exactly the point where a screen makes bedtime worse.
> The fix is structural rather than heroic. Move the outdoor block to the morning and treat daylight as the scarce resource it now is. Keep something specifically for the dark hour that she likes and doesn't get otherwise — the light table, a torch, a bath earlier than usual. And expect the whole family to be worse at this time of day for a couple of months. It isn't a discipline problem; it's a season.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Four o'clock in November is the hardest hour of the year and it isn't anybody's fault. Bath early, lights low, and don't try to be impressive.
