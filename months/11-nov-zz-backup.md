# 🌧️ November — Backup Days & Extra Ideas

These pages are for the November days the plan does not survive. Gray at
breakfast, cold rain that sets in before eight and never lifts, a temperature
on a small forehead, an afternoon gone dark by five. The two bad weather days
are full plans built to run with the door shut, on what the house already
holds, because the weather is the reason nobody is going to the store.
The two sick days ask almost nothing of her and not much more of you, and the
last page is eleven ideas at a time for the mornings when you have ten minutes
and no plan.

---

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 1 — Three Colors and the Gray Outside

Cold rain since before you woke up, and the forecast has it staying. November
hands her one long gray view out of every window in the house, so today you
paint it, and the whole afternoon happens on the floor under the lamp from
one o'clock because the dark is back by five. Lunch at twelve stays small on
purpose, because the floor is being set for one o'clock.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Stand the paints, two jars for water and a stack of newspaper on the table, and put an empty egg carton and the widest brush you own beside them.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: Nine Grays in an Egg Carton
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Street in Three Colors (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Getting Warm Without a Coat (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: The Picnic That Never Went Outside (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🏠 Indoors Instead: **Pick one** (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: The Curtains Across Every Window
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Grays That Are Left
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Warm Bath While It Keeps Raining
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Nine Grays in an Egg Carton

**🧰 You need:** 1 empty egg carton · white and black paint (blue and brown will do) · 1 spoon · 1 brush.

1. A spoon of white into each cup along the row. She does the spooning and it will not be even, which changes nothing.
2. One touch of black into the second cup, two into the third, and on down the row. She stirs each one with the handle of the brush and watches the row darken under her hands.
3. Carry the carton to the window and hold it up. Find the cup that matches the sky, then the roof, then the wet road. Leave the carton wet on the table.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Street in Three Colors

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The egg carton of grays
- 1 large sheet of thick paper, or the flat side of a cut-open box
- White paint, and one more color she picks
- 1 wide brush and 1 thin brush
- 1 jar of water and 1 old dish towel
- Newspaper under the whole lot
- 1 permanent marker

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Two minutes at the window first. Ask her what is out there and take the list as it comes, gate and puddle and the neighbor's van included.
2. Say the rule out loud and put the rest of the paints back in the box in front of her: the grays, white, and one color of her choosing. She picks the color before a brush gets wet.
3. Big shapes first with the wide brush. Sky across the top, road along the bottom, and she covers the paper before anything gets detail. The brush goes through the jar of water and dries on the old dish towel between colors.
4. Then her list, in gray. Roofs, the van, the bare tree, the fence, whatever she named. Thin brush, and she works from the top down so her sleeve stays out of it.
5. The one color goes last and it has to be spent. She decides what deserves it: a front door, an umbrella, one lit window.
6. Prop it against the window to dry and go and look at it from the far side of the room. Write the date along the bottom in marker while she watches.

> 💡 **Tip:** The single color is the whole activity. Open the full set and you get a brown puddle by nine-forty, and she never once has to decide.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Getting Warm Without a Coat

The house is cold and she has not moved her whole body since breakfast. Clear
the hallway and build one round out of six things: ten jumping jacks, ten
stomps, ten arm circles, a lap of the living room on tiptoe, the laundry basket
carried the length of the hall, and a slow crawl back under the low table. Do
the round with her and count out loud. At the end of each one she puts the back
of her hand against her own cheek and says whether she is warm yet. The answer
changes by the third round. Give her the marker and the back of yesterday's
envelope and let her mark each round off as she finishes it. Five rounds takes
forty minutes with the arguing about tiptoe included, and she comes to lunch
pink and loud and ready to sit down.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 17 months and there is no version of
> this morning where he holds a loaded brush. Give him his own brush, a cup of
> plain water and a flattened box on the floor at 9:15, and let him paint the
> cardboard dark. It dries and he does it again. He walks the hallway rounds
> at 10:30 too, a lap behind her, stomping because she is stomping. Two-word
> combinations are starting, so expect *more water* the second the cup runs
> out.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Picnic That Never Went Outside

Morning was wet paint and standing up. Everybody sits on the floor now, the
curtains come across, and nobody stands up again until the tray goes back.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 blanket or an old sheet
- Cushions, one per person
- Real plates and cups, not the picnic set
- 1 tray
- Cold food the house already has: crackers, cheese, fruit, carrot sticks, dry cereal, a can of peaches, whatever is open in the fridge
- 1 small pitcher, half filled
- 2 dish towels
- 1 lamp

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. She clears the middle of the living room floor. Move the low table out together and let her decide which way around the blanket goes.
2. She lays a place for everybody in the house, plus two toys she chooses, and everyone gets a real plate.
3. Food onto the tray, cold, no stove involved: what lunch left, plus whatever is open in the fridge. She loads it and carries it in two hands, one trip, and you walk behind her.
4. Curtains across, big light off, lamp on. The room changes and she will notice before you say anything.
5. Everybody down on the floor with a cushion each, adults included, and nobody gets up for forty minutes. Phones go on the shelf.
6. She pours. Small pitcher, half full, one cup at a time, and a spill gets a dish towel rather than a comment.
7. Clearing is part of it. Everything back on the tray, blanket shaken out over the bath, cushions returned.

> 💡 **Tip:** Stay down. The picnic works because you are at her eye level for an hour, and it stops working the moment you start doing the dishes behind her.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Blanket over the kitchen table instead, and eat underneath it.
- Skip the food and take the books down there.
- One plate each, laid out on the floor in color order.
- Move the whole thing to the upstairs hallway, where the light is different.

### 🏠 Indoors Instead: **Pick one**
- **Hang the morning's paintings** along the hallway at her height, tape at all four corners
- Empty the coat closet and sit in it with a lamp
- Everybody's shoes in one line, biggest to smallest, front door to kitchen
- A phone call to whoever is furthest away, with three questions she prepares
- The linen closet laundry, sorted into piles by who it belongs to

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Kreston is 17 months and a blanket on the floor
> is the best table he has met. Sit him at the edge with his own plate and cup,
> and expect him to walk through the middle of it twice carrying something.
> Give him the second dish towel to wipe with. He copies wiping now and does a
> worse job than the spill.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A constraint is what makes her choose instead of asking for everything.**
> Open the whole paint box and Azlyn uses every color inside five minutes, and
> the paper goes brown. Hand her three and something else happens. She holds
> the red back, looks at the roof, looks at the door, and decides which one is
> worth it, and that decision is the thinking you were hoping for. Three-year-
> olds get almost no practice at choosing, because most of the day arrives
> already chosen for them. A small fixed set gives her a real decision with a
> visible result at the end of it. The limit does the teaching, and it leaves
> you free to say yes to everything inside it.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Paint water looks like drinking water to a 17-month-old, so
> the jars stay on the table and go down the sink the moment the brushes are
> washed. Use washable non-toxic paint only, because Kreston will get a
> brushful in his mouth at some point today. Wipe any drip off the floor as it
> happens; a wet floorboard with two children running on it is the real hazard
> in this room.

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 2 — The Pot That Cooks All Day

Gray sky, cold rain going sideways and no reason to open the door. Today she
does the chopping with a knife that works, dinner goes on the stove before
eleven and fills the house for six hours, and the afternoon takes something
apart on a towel while the light drains out of the windows.

### 🌙 Prep Tonight
> Stand six things from the fridge and the cupboard on the counter, find the small serrated knife and the cutting board, and dig out one dead battery-powered thing nobody will miss.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **8:00–9:00 AM** — 🌅 Opening Activity: The Thumb Test
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting (15 min)
- **9:15–10:15 AM** — 🎨 The Main Event: The Bridge and the Claw (60 min)
- **10:15–10:30 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:30–11:15 AM** — 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Knocking the Bottles Down (45 min)
- **11:15–11:45 AM** — Free Play (30 min)
- **11:45 AM–12:00 PM** — 🤸 Wiggle Time (15 min)
- **12:00–12:30 PM** — Lunch
- **12:30–1:00 PM** — 📚 Book Time
- **1:00–2:30 PM** — 🎨 Second Main Event: What's Inside the Broken Thing (90 min)
- **2:30–3:30 PM** — 🏠 Indoors Instead: **Pick one** (60 min)
- **3:30–4:00 PM** — Snack & Free Play
- **4:00–4:15 PM** — 🧹 Her Job: A Bowl at Every Place
- **4:15–5:00 PM** — Quiet Play: The Parts Poster
- **5:00–5:45 PM** — 🛁 Wind-Down: Bath While the House Smells of Dinner
- **6:00 PM** — **Dinner**

### 🌅 Opening Activity: The Thumb Test

**🧰 You need:** 6 things out of the fridge and the cupboard, 4 of them soft enough to take a thumbprint (mushroom, zucchini, banana, a ripe pear, canned peach halves, a slice of bread) and 2 that will not (onion, carrot, potato, a hard apple) · 1 tray.

1. Line them all along the counter and give her one instruction: press a thumb into each one, hard.
2. Two piles. The ones that give, and the ones that fight back. She sorts, you say nothing.
3. Tell her the giving pile is hers at 9:15 and the fighting pile is yours. Leave both on the tray.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Bridge and the Claw

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 small serrated table knife with a rounded tip (**adult supervision the whole time**)
- 1 cutting board and 1 damp dish towel to sit it on
- Her pile, the four that took a thumbprint
- Your pile, the two that did not (**yours**)
- 1 large bowl
- 1 heavy pot with a lid
- 1 can of tomatoes, or a jar of pasta sauce, or two spoons of tomato paste and an extra cup of water
- Oil, salt, 1 cup of water or stock
- 1 can of beans or a cup of dried lentils, if the six things looked thin in the bowl

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Damp towel under the board so it cannot slide. She stands at the counter with the board at elbow height, or sits at the table if the counter is too high.
2. Bridge grip first, on the softest thing in her pile. Her free hand arches over the top like a bridge, fingers down each side, and the blade goes through the gap under it. Show it once, say it once, then keep your hands off.
3. Claw grip next, on the longest thing she has, laid with a flat side down so it stays put. Knuckles forward, fingertips curled back, and the flat of the blade rests against her knuckles as it comes down.
4. One piece of food at a time and no hurrying her along. Every piece goes in the bowl, uneven, thick, fine, and it all cooks the same.
5. You take your two, standing beside her, because both of them roll. She watches and calls out which grip you are using.
6. Everything into the pot with the tomatoes, the oil, the salt and a cup of water. Tip the beans or the lentils in as well if the bowl came up short. Lid on, lowest heat, and it stays there until five o'clock.

> 💡 **Tip:** She never carries the knife anywhere. It lives on the board, it goes back on the board between every piece, and it comes off the board into your hand when the chopping stops.

### 🏠 Instead of Getting Outside: Knocking the Bottles Down

Ten empty bottles and cans out of the recycling, stood in a triangle at the
far end of the hallway, and one soft ball. She rolls from a taped line, counts
what falls and stands them back up herself, which is half the game. Move the
line back a step every time she clears more than five. Give her the marker and
the envelope again if she wants to keep score.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 17 months and kicks a ball now, so he
> gets the same triangle and no line at all. He will walk up to the bottles and
> kick from a foot away, and that counts. Stand the bottles up for him as fast
> as he flattens them, and give him the word *down* every time, because two-
> word combinations are starting and *all down* is one he can reach.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: What's Inside the Broken Thing

Morning was sharp, careful and standing at the counter. This afternoon she
sits down with a screwdriver and finds out what has been inside the radio the
whole time.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 dead battery-powered object: a radio, a flashlight, a toy that stopped working, a wind-up clock
- 1 medium Phillips screwdriver and 1 small flathead
- 1 muffin tin or egg carton, for the screws
- 1 old towel and 1 tray
- 1 zip-top bag
- 1 piece of card, 1 marker, masking tape

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Batteries out first, in front of her, and up on a high shelf. You open nothing that plugs into the wall and nothing with a glass screen.
2. Towel on the table, tray on the towel. Everything that comes off the object lands on the tray and stays there.
3. Hunt the screws together. She points at each one, you break it loose with the Phillips, and she turns it out the rest of the way with both hands on the handle. The small flathead does the slotted screws and pries the case apart at the seam.
4. Every screw goes straight into one cup of the muffin tin. Count them in and count them out at the end.
5. Open it up. One part out at a time, laid along the tray biggest to smallest, and she does the lifting.
6. Ask what each part might do and write her answer on the card as she says it. The speaker cone is a drum and the spring is a snake, and both answers go down word for word.
7. Tape three parts to the card as a poster for her wall. The rest go in the zip-top bag with the screws, on the high shelf.

> 💡 **Tip:** Turning a screw out is heavy work for a three-year-old wrist and she will manage two out of six. Break each one loose for her and let the finishing be hers.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- One screw only, then leave the rest and come back tomorrow.
- Take the batteries out of everything in the house and line them up by size.
- Draw round each part on paper instead of taping it down.
- Open an old ballpoint pen, which comes apart in four pieces and needs no tools.

### 🏠 Indoors Instead: **Pick one**
- **Taste the pot** with a teaspoon each, and let her say what it still needs
- The parts poster taped up in the hallway at her height
- Bottles back down the hall, this time bowling underhand from the kitchen door
- A blanket over two dining chairs and the parts tray carried inside
- Bread out of the cupboard, torn and stacked ready for the bowls at six

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Kreston is 17 months and every part on that tray
> is choke-size. He is out of this room while it is open, in the hallway with
> the bottles and a door between, or asleep. Bring him back once the bag is
> shut and the tray is cleared, and give him the empty muffin tin and a wooden
> spoon at floor level.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A sharp-enough knife is safer than a blunt one, and she can learn a grip.**
> A blunt knife slides. It skates off a tomato skin and lands in the hand
> holding the tomato, which is hers, because she leaned on it harder to make it
> work. A small serrated knife bites where she puts it, so she uses less force
> and keeps the line she aimed at. The grip carries more of the safety than the
> blade does. The bridge, her hand arched over the food with the blade passing
> underneath, keeps her fingers above the edge. The claw, knuckles forward and
> fingertips tucked back, gives the blade a wall to lean on. Show each one
> once, then sit beside her with your hands in your lap.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** You are within arm's reach of that knife for every second it
> is out of the drawer, and it goes back in your hand and into the drawer the
> moment the chopping ends. Never leave it on the board while you cross the
> room. Kreston stays out of the kitchen while she cuts and while the pot is
> on: the handle turns inward, the pot sits on a back burner, and the lowest
> heat still burns a hand that reaches. On the take-apart, batteries come out
> before the first screw, you open nothing that plugs into a wall socket, and
> you count the screws and small parts into the tin and bag them at the end.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1 — The Worst Day of It

This is the day she cannot lift her head off the cushion, and there is nothing
in this book she needs. The plan is a warm room, one adult within reach and a
long sleep in the middle of the afternoon. You can stop any of it halfway
through and lose nothing, and none of it asks her to cut, build or leave the
spot you make for her.

### 🌙 Before You Come Downstairs
> Fill a small cup and stand it where her hand falls without her lifting her head. Put a bowl of cool water and two washcloths on the low table by the sofa, and her own pillow and the blanket she recognizes at the end of it.

### 🕗 The Shape of the Day

> *No clock times today. She sets the order and you follow her.*

- **Whenever she wakes.** Downstairs in her pajamas, blanket, and a drink already poured within her reach.
- **Before the first long sleep.** The cool cloth round, then one story with her eyes shut.
- **The long sleep.** The rest block, and it owns as much of the day as it wants.
- **When she asks for food.** Dry, plain and small, on a saucer rather than a plate.
- **Before the afternoon dip.** The sips, counted, and the same book as many times as she asks for it.
- **When the light goes.** Lamps low, bath skipped, bed early and yours if that is what she wants.

### 🛏️ The Rest Block

The middle of this day is a sleep, and it is the point of the day rather than
a gap in it. Make the sofa the bed: her own pillow off her bed, a blanket she
recognizes, the curtains across. Sit at the end of it with your hand on her
leg. She has dropped her nap for a year and she will fight the idea for four
minutes and go under for two hours. Take the two hours. Answer nothing that
can wait, and do not fill the room with the sound of the dishwasher being
emptied above her head.

### 🫗 Sips She Counts Herself

One small cup, filled a third of the way, and a straw if you have one. Set it
where her hand falls without her lifting her head. The job is little and
often, so every time you pass you say *one sip*, she takes one, and you both
count how many she has managed since the last sleep. Water, watered juice,
flat soda at room temperature, whatever she will take. Ice chips work on a day
when a cup is too much effort.

### 📖 The Story With Her Eyes Shut

Books are heavy today and her eyes ache. Tell her a story about herself
instead, with her eyes closed: the morning she found the frost on the gate,
the day the tower came down, the muddiest puddle on the lane. Slow, quiet, and
full of the things she recognizes. She will correct you when you get a detail
wrong, and that correction is the most she will do all day.

### 🧊 The Cool Cloth Round

The bowl and the two washcloths are on the low table from last night, so this
one starts where she is lying. Wring one out, lay it across her forehead,
count to twenty and swap it for the other. Wipe her hands and the back of her
neck. Three rounds, then the bowl goes down the sink and the cloths go over
the rail. It lowers nothing on a thermometer and it settles her, which is the
job.

> 👶 **Where Kreston Goes:** A sick sibling changes his day too. If a
> grandparent or a friend can take him from breakfast until dinner, this is the
> day to ask, and ask early. Failing that, run him in the next room with the
> door open and a gate across, his own cup that never crosses the doorway, and
> the toys that need no help. Wash your hands between the two of them every
> single time. He is 17 months and will climb on her if he gets the chance, so
> he does not sit on the sofa today.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Dark afternoons change the shape of the day, and the plan has to change with them.**
> Sunset comes before five for most of November, and the light sets the shape
> of a sick day here. Her body reads a fading afternoon as a signal. Melatonin
> arrives ahead of schedule, and the sleep pressure a fever already built
> climbs on top of it. You have the long sleep in the middle of the day and the
> lamps low by four, so the day ends where her body wants it to end. Bed before
> six counts as a bedtime this month. A child running a temperature spends most
> of her fuel on the fever, and an evening two hours short hands some of it
> back to her. Tomorrow starts early, and that suits her too.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** An open bowl of water on the floor is a drowning risk for a
> 17-month-old, so the cool bowl lives on the low table beside her and goes
> down the sink the moment the third round ends. Call your pediatrician the
> same day for any of these: a fever in a child this age that lasts beyond
> three days, breathing that is fast or noisy or pulls the skin in at her
> ribs, no wet diaper or trip to the bathroom in eight hours, a headache with
> a stiff neck, a rash that stays visible when you press a glass against it,
> or a child too drowsy to wake. Trust the pattern you know. Write down the
> time and the dose of anything you give her on a piece of paper on the
> counter, because a second adult on a long day is the way double doses
> happen. Ice goes in only while she sits up, crushed small under the back of
> a spoon, and whole cubes stay in the freezer, because a cube slides back on
> a flat child and blocks her airway.

## 🤒 Sick Day 2 — The Day She Has Plans Again

The fever broke overnight, she is asking for things again, and she is nowhere
near ready to be back at it. This is the harder of the two days, because she
has energy for about eleven minutes at a stretch and none at all after that.
She does all of it lying down, on a lap or from under a blanket, one hand, no
setup.

### 🌙 Before You Come Downstairs
> Stand four teaspoons, a towel and a sheet of paper on the low table by the sofa, with the blanket and the book she knows by heart. Leave the drawer you plan to bring her by the door.

### 🕗 The Shape of the Day

> *No clock times today. She sets the order and you follow her.*

- **Whenever she wakes.** She will be brighter than yesterday and want the whole day at once. Slow it down before it starts.
- **After breakfast.** Four spoons, four words, and a small honest taste of food.
- **When she starts asking for jobs.** One drawer on the blanket, and it can stop after two minutes.
- **The rest block.** The sleep she says she does not need, and she still needs it.
- **Before the afternoon dip.** The list for when she is better, dictated by her.
- **When the light goes.** Warm water and a washcloth where she is lying, then a longer wind-down than usual.

### 🛏️ The Rest Block

She will argue about this one today, which is how you know she is on the mend.
Hold it anyway. Same sofa, same blanket, curtains across, and a rule that she
does not have to sleep and does have to stay lying down. Put the book she
knows by heart in her hands and go quiet. Most children are asleep by the
third page. A recovering three-year-old who skips this sleeps in bursts, then
crashes at four thirty, and tomorrow starts a day behind.

### 🥄 Four Spoons and Four Words

Four teaspoons, one taste on each, done from where she is lying with a towel
under her chin. A smear of honey on the first, a lick of salt on the second, a
drop of lemon juice or vinegar on the third, a scrape of unsweetened cocoa
powder on the fourth. Name each one as it goes in her mouth: sweet, salty,
sour, bitter. She will pull a face at the lemon and ask for it twice more.
Then the game: eyes shut, you load a spoon, and she names which of the four it
was. Nothing to clear up afterward but four spoons.

### 🗄️ One Drawer on the Blanket

Bring her a drawer. The kitchen drawer with the odds in it, the button tin,
the sock basket, the bag of bottle caps. Tip it on the blanket over her legs
and let her sort it into whatever groups she names, or leave it in a heap and
sort three things. This is a lap-sized job with a floor of two minutes and a
ceiling of forty, and either is a fine result today.

### 📝 The List for When She's Better

She has been indoors for days and she is full of plans. Take them down. Sit
with a sheet of paper and write the whole list as she says it, word for word,
including the trip to the moon. Read it back to her twice and pin it where she
can see it. Tomorrow, or the day after, she picks one off the list and you do
it, so keep at least one of them small enough to happen.

> 👶 **Where Kreston Goes:** He can come back into the room today, in short
> doses, with one rule: he stays on the floor and she stays on the sofa. He is
> 17 months and will want to climb up beside her within a minute. Give him a
> job at her feet instead, posting balled socks back into the laundry basket
> one at a time while she directs him from above. The button tin and the
> bottle caps stay up on the sofa with her and come down only while he is out
> of the room. Twenty minutes of that is plenty for both of them.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **She can name what she doesn't like once she has words for the tastes.**
> A recovering three-year-old turns food down and cannot tell you what is wrong
> with it. Sour, salty, sweet and bitter give her four handles. Put a smear of
> each on a spoon, name it while it is in her mouth, and inside a week *yuck*
> turns into *too sour*, which is something you can cook around. Her tongue is
> more sensitive than yours, bitter most of all, and that is why broccoli and
> medicine land harder for her than they do for you. Words for the tastes also
> give her a way to accept a thing she dislikes, because a thing with a name is
> a smaller thing.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Keep her home while a fever is still coming back in the
> afternoons, and for a full day after the last one, whatever the calendar
> says. Written doses stay on the counter, and the same rule holds today:
> nobody gives anything without reading the sheet first. Watch for the second
> illness that arrives on the back of the first, because a child who was mending
> and goes downhill again gets a fresh call to the pediatrician rather than
> another day on the sofa.

## 🎲 November — Extra Ideas

Ten minutes, no plan, cold rain on the glass and the lamps on at four. Pick a

### 🎲 Alternative Activities
- **Fifty Steps Around the House**, counted out loud, then fifty back the other way.
- **The Mug She Carries Herself**, she picks it, stirs the warm milk and walks it to her own chair.
- **The Radiator Race**, wet mittens and hats laid out and checked to see which dries first.
- **Everything Blue in Four Minutes**, a timed hunt with a laundry basket and no rules about what counts.
- **The Chair Tunnel**, four dining chairs in a row under a blanket, with a job waiting at each end.
- **The Bell in the Next Room**, eyes shut, and she points at where the sound came from.
- **Two Minutes of Standing Still**, a contest nobody wins and everybody enters.
- **The Sofa Cushion Wall**, every cushion off the furniture and built into a wall across one doorway.
- **The Long Roll Down the Hallway**, a ball pushed back and forth sitting down, legs apart.
- **The Sock Skate Race**, socks on the smooth floor and the length of the kitchen crossed sliding.
- **The Newspaper Mountain**, a whole newspaper torn into strips and stuffed into a pillowcase.

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts
- **Rain Prints**, a sheet of paper held out under a drip for ten seconds and dried flat.
- **The Window Sign for the Street**, one big word drawn thick and taped facing out for whoever walks past.
- **Bubble Wrap Printing**, paint rolled over the bubbles and pressed onto paper.
- **The Foil Puddle**, foil pressed into a saucer and flooded with watered-down color.
- **Coffee Grounds Mud Paint**, used grounds stirred into glue for a brown that smells.
- **The Torn Paper Tree**, brown strips for a trunk and bare branches, because that is what is out there.
- **Candle Stub Lines**, wax scribbles that the paint refuses to cover.
- **The Odd Sock Draft Stopper**, stuffed with the rest of the odd socks and given a face.
- **Cotton Swab Dots**, one color per swab and no brushes allowed.
- **The Envelope Street**, old envelopes cut into lit windows and glued along a dark sheet.
- **Potato Stamps**, cut by you and printed by her onto a paper bag for wrapping.

### 👶 Kreston's Turn
- **The Ball Down the Hallway**, you sitting at one end, him kicking from the other.
- **Two Words at the Window**, *car gone* and *rain wet*, handed back to him each time he points.
- **Two Cups and the Clothespins**, dropped from one cup into the other and tipped out to start again.
- **The Pan Lid Drum**, one wooden spoon and one lid, at floor level, for as long as you can stand it.
