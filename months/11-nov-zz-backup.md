# 🌧️ November Backup Days
## *For when the plan doesn't survive the morning*

**November's version of a bad day:** it's dark, it's wet, and it's been dark and wet for a fortnight. This is the month where the outdoor block genuinely can't happen some days, and where the whole household is more tired than anyone quite accounts for. There's also a long stretch of grey between the excitement of Halloween and the excitement of December, and children feel that flatness as much as adults do.

Two of these are for weather that has made the decision for you, and two are for the coughs and colds that go round from now until March.

---

<a id="nov-weather-1"></a>

## 🌧️ Bad Weather Day 1: The Grey Wet Nothing Day
**Use when:** It isn't dramatic, it's just relentlessly grey and wet and everyone is flat.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting: Build the Den First (15 min)
- **9:15–10:30 AM** — Main Event: The Post Office in the Den (75 min)
- **10:30–10:45 AM** — Snack: **in the den** (15 min)
- **10:45–11:30 AM** — Indoor Movement: The Obstacle Delivery Route (45 min)
- **11:30 AM–12:00 PM** — Quiet Play (30 min)
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch & Nap Prep

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Post Office in the Den
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- Sheets, blankets, and every cushion in the house
- Clothes pegs or clips, to hold the sheets
- A torch, or fairy lights
- Paper, envelopes, and old cards
- Stickers, markers, and tape
- A tote bag, for the post
- A shoebox with a slot cut in the lid

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Build the den first**, before anything else is decided. **Sofa cushions, sheets over chairs, pegs at the corners.** She directs; you do the high bits.
2. **Light it** — torch or battery fairy lights inside. **A lit den on a grey morning is worth more than any activity you could plan.**
3. **Then it becomes a post office.** The shoebox is the postbox, the tote is the mailbag, and the den is the sorting office.
4. **She writes letters to everyone in the house.** Drawings count. **Ask what each one says and write her words underneath.**
5. **Address them, stamp them with stickers, post them.**
6. **Then the delivery round**, which is the movement session.
7. **Real letters at the end:** one addressed properly, to a grandparent or a friend, that actually goes out when the weather breaks.

> 💡 **Tip:** Don't dismantle the den at lunchtime. On a day like this it's the whole afternoon as well, and it can be a reading nest, a cave, a shop, and a bed for the toys without any further input from you.

### 🏃 Indoor Movement: The Obstacle Delivery Route
Set up a route round the house with obstacles — under the table, over the cushions, along a taped line, round a chair — and she has to deliver each letter without leaving the course. **Time it. Then do it backwards.**

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is around 18 months and the den is the best thing that has ever happened to him. Give him the shoebox and a stack of old cards to post, which he'll do for as long as anyone will hand them back.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **A den changes the day more than any activity in it.**
> On a flat, grey, indoors morning, the instinct is to find a better activity. But the thing most likely to reset a stuck day isn't what she does — it's changing where she is. A small, enclosed, dimly lit space that she built herself makes ordinary things feel different.
> Small children seek out enclosed spaces for a reason: they're calming, they reduce the amount of room to attend to, and they're one of the few environments a four-year-old gets to control completely. Which is why the same drawing, story, or snack lands better inside a sheet fort than at the table. When nothing is working in November, change the room rather than the plan.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Same crayons, same paper, under a sheet. Two hours instead of ten minutes. I don't fully understand it either.

## 💨 Bad Weather Day 2: The Horizontal Rain Day
**Use when:** It's blowing a gale and lashing down, and even the doorstep is out of the question.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **9:00–9:15 AM** — Morning Meeting: Listen to It (15 min)
- **9:15–10:30 AM** — Main Event: The Bath Laboratory (75 min)
- **10:30–10:45 AM** — Snack (15 min)
- **10:45–11:30 AM** — Indoor Movement: The Weather Dance (45 min)
- **11:30 AM–12:00 PM** — Quiet Play (30 min)
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch & Nap Prep

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Bath Laboratory
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The bath, run shallow and warm
- Jugs, funnels, sieves, a turkey baster, a whisk
- Plastic bottles with holes punched at different heights — **adult punches**
- A length of hosepipe or plastic tubing
- Food colouring
- A few things that float and a few that don't
- Towels, plenty, and a bath mat outside it

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Shallow warm bath, swimming costume or nothing, and a full set of kit.** This is not a wash — say that at the start or she'll expect to get out in ten minutes.
2. **The fountain bottle:** three holes punched up the side of a bottle. Fill it and watch — **the bottom hole squirts furthest.** Then fill it again and watch the jets change as it empties.
3. **The siphon**, which is the best trick in the bath. **Fill the tube completely, hold both ends, put one in the bath and one in a bowl on the floor below.** Water climbs up and over and keeps going on its own. **She will make you do this repeatedly.**
4. **Pouring station:** funnels, sieves, jugs, and the turkey baster, which is the single best water toy there is.
5. **A drop of colouring** in one jug so she can watch it move and mix.
6. **Whisk up a bubble mountain** and hide things in it.
7. **Get out before she's cold**, and wrap her up. Forty-five minutes is usually the limit.

> ⚠️ **Never leave her alone in the bath**, not for a second, not on a day when the point of it is that it's absorbing. Shallow water, adult in the room, whole time.

> 💡 **Tip:** A bath at ten in the morning is a genuine mood-reset on a wild day, and it means bath time in the evening is off the list. Both of those are worth having.

### 🏃 Indoor Movement: The Weather Dance
Be the weather. Rain — fingers tapping the floor, fast. Wind — running in circles, arms out. Thunder — stamping. Sun — lying flat and still. **Call them out in a random order and everyone changes.** Then she calls them.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston gets in with her, if that works in your house, or takes his turn afterwards. **Water play at 18 months is one of the best things available to him** — a washing-up bowl on a towel on the floor if the bath is busy.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Water is the most reliable regulator you have, and it works in either direction.**
> Warm water settles a wound-up child and it revives a flat one, which is unusual — most things do one or the other. It's absorbing without being stimulating, it requires nothing of her, and it will hold attention for forty-five minutes on a day when nothing else holds it for five.
> Which makes it worth treating as a tool rather than a chore. A morning bath on a bad day, a washing-up bowl on the kitchen floor, a sink of warm water and some cups — these aren't filler. They're the most dependable option in the book for a day that has gone wrong, and the fact that they're free and available in November is exactly why they belong in the backup section.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Bath at ten in the morning. Completely different child by eleven.

## 🤒 Sick Day 1: The Long Cold
**Use when:** She's streaming, coughing, exhausted, and it's the third day of it.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Nest, warm drink, no demands (30 min)
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Post-It Story Wall (20–40 min, in bursts)
- **Snack** — Warm, soft, and plenty to drink
- **Late morning** — Wind-Down: Steam and stories
- **Nap early**, and let it run as long as it wants

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Post-It Story Wall
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A pad of sticky notes
- A marker for you, crayons for her
- A wall, a door, or the side of the sofa she can see from the nest
- Cushions, blankets, and tissues within reach
- A drink with a straw
- A bowl of warm water for steam, and a towel

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Build the nest so she doesn't have to move again**, then put the sticky notes where she can see them.
2. **One idea per note, and she doesn't have to write any of them.** She says it, you write it, it goes on the wall.
3. **Start with a character.** Then a place. Then a problem. **One note at a time, with gaps in between where nothing happens.**
4. **The wall is the point** — she can see the story building without holding any of it in her head, which is exactly right when she's ill.
5. **Move the notes around.** Ask what happens if this bit goes first. **Rearranging is easier than inventing** and she can do it from where she's lying.
6. **When there are eight or ten, read the whole wall out loud in order**, with voices.
7. **Leave it up.** Add to it tomorrow if she's still poorly.

> 💡 **Tip:** Let the gaps be long. A poorly four-year-old will do two minutes, stop, watch nothing for a quarter of an hour, and then come back with the best idea of the day. Filling the gap ends it.

### 😴 Wind-Down: Steam and Stories
A bowl of hot water on a surface **well out of reach**, so the room gets a bit of steam in it, and a story in the dark. Steam won't cure a cold but a humid room genuinely helps a blocked-up child sleep.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** If Kreston has it too, he's 18 months and miserable and wants carrying. Carry him. Put both of them in the nest and write the day off completely.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **The third day of a cold is the worst one for both of you.**
> The first day she's obviously ill and everyone lowers their expectations. By the third she's still streaming and coughing but no longer acutely unwell — bored, whiny, clingy, and not up to anything — and the sympathy in the house has quietly run out.
> Knowing that's a predictable shape helps, because it stops the third day being read as her being difficult. What she needs isn't more entertainment, it's less: fewer transitions, less talking, one thing she can do lying down, and someone nearby not asking anything of her. Colds in a small child routinely last ten to fourteen days. The middle of that is a slog and it is not a sign anything has gone wrong.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Day three is the hard one every single time and I forget every single time.

## 🤧 Sick Day 2: Back on Her Feet
**Use when:** She's better but not right — enough energy to want things, not enough to do them.

### ⏰ At-a-Glance Schedule
- **Whenever she wakes** — Slow start, her pace (30 min)
- **Mid-morning** — Main Event: The Kitchen Table Café (30–45 min)
- **Snack** — **The café's first customer**
- **Late morning** — **The Doorstep Ten Minutes**, if she's up to it
- **Wind-Down** — Audio story
- **Nap whenever she'll take it**

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Kitchen Table Café
**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- A tray, and a cloth
- Bread, butter, a banana, crackers, cheese
- Her knife and board from Week 48, if she's steady enough — **or a spreader if she isn't**
- Card and markers, for the menu
- A jug of water and cups
- An apron
- A small notepad, for orders

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. **Everything happens sitting down at the table.** Set it up so she never needs to stand.
2. **She writes the menu first** — three things, drawn. Prices in dots if she wants them.
3. **Take an order** and let her write it however she writes.
4. **She makes it.** Spreading butter, slicing a banana, arranging crackers. **Small, low, achievable jobs that produce a real thing.**
5. **She serves it on the tray**, and you eat it properly and say what you think.
6. **Then she takes an order from Kreston**, who will order by pointing, and serves him too.
7. **Two or three customers is a full shift.** Close the café before she's tired of it.

> 💡 **Tip:** Recovery appetite is unreliable, so the café is a good way in — she'll often eat what she's made and served when she'd refuse the same food on a plate.

### 🌳 Get Outside: The Doorstep Ten Minutes
**Ten minutes, coat on, on the step or at the gate.** Not a walk. Just outside air and a look at the sky and the tree from Day 324. **Fresh air genuinely helps a recovering child**, and ten minutes is enough to change the whole afternoon.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is the customer and he's delighted about it. He'll want to be served repeatedly, which suits her perfectly.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Coming back to normal needs a smaller version of normal first.**
> The temptation on the first good day is to resume — the full plan, the proper outing, the activity that got missed. It usually ends in tears by eleven, because recovery energy is real but shallow and runs out without warning.
> A miniature version of an ordinary day works far better: a small job with a beginning and an end, ten minutes of outside instead of an hour, one person to interact with rather than a room. She gets the satisfaction of doing something real and doesn't hit the wall. The full day comes back tomorrow, and it comes back faster if today was small on purpose.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** Two rounds of buttered toast and ten minutes on the doorstep, and she was back to herself by teatime.

## ✨ November — Extra Ideas
### *When you just need one more thing*

---

### 🎲 Alternative Activities

**Check the Weather Station** — The gauge and the chart from Week 46 are still out. Five minutes, every morning, all winter.

**Visit the Tree** — Day 324's tree, any time. Photo from the same spot, and one line in the file.

**Check the Bulbs** — Nothing will be happening. Check anyway; that's the lesson.

**The Rotting Jar** — One look a week, one mark on the chart. Thirty seconds.

**Butter in a Jar** — Cream, a jar, ten minutes of shaking. Works every time and always gets a reaction.

**Washing Up** — Genuinely an activity at this age. Warm water, two bowls, and a job that finishes.

**The Sheepdog Whistles** — Four signals, one runner. Good in a park, good in a corridor, good for burning an hour.

**Torch Walk** — Dark by half four. Two torches, twenty minutes, and it counts as the outdoor block.

**The Log Pile** — Add to it. Sticks, leaves, anything. It'll be full of sleeping things by January.

**Bird Watching from the Window** — Colder weather brings more of them to the garden. Clipboard, three columns, tally.

**Kreston's Turn** — He's 17–18 months. A washing-up bowl of water on a towel with cups and a funnel, a basket of pinecones to move from room to room, and a low box to climb into. Rotate all three.

---

### 🎨 Alternative Arts & Crafts

**Ice Suncatchers** — Leaves and berries in a dish of water, outside overnight. Works from now until March.

**Cotton Wool Clouds** — Three types, blue card, correct names underneath.

**Rainbow Making** — CD and a torch. Ten seconds of setup, and it never stops being good.

**Bark Rubbings** — From Day 324's tree. Same tree, every season, same page.

**Seed Packets** — Fold, fill, label, date. Fine motor work with a purpose attached.

**Wool Twisting** — A pinch of roving and two fingers. Ten minutes, and it makes string.

**Salt Dough Ornaments** — Start now if December is going to involve any. They need a week to dry properly.

**Handprint Turkey** — Traditional, quick, and it goes in the keepsake box.

**Weather Symbols** — Card, markers, sticky tack. Makes the fridge into a forecast board.

**Pressed Leaves, Last Chance** — The good ones have mostly gone by now. Grab what's left.

**Menu Cards** — For any meal. Drawn, priced in dots, and propped in the middle of the table.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **November is the flattest month, and lowering the plan is the right response.**
> There's no festival, no holiday to build towards after the first fortnight, the light has gone, and everyone in the house has had a cold. It's the month where a book like this one is least likely to survive contact with the day, and where the gap between the plan and reality feels worst.
> The useful thing to know is that the flatness is seasonal rather than personal. Nobody has stopped trying and nothing has gone wrong. What helps is cutting the plan roughly in half, getting outside in the morning whenever the light allows, keeping one small daily ritual going — the weather chart, the tree, the bulbs — and letting the rest slide. December arrives on its own and brings its own energy with it.

> 💛 **From Joseph:** If November is the month this book gets ignored, that's fine. It was always meant to be a suggestion, not a debt.
