## Week 2: Nursery Rhymes & Rhythm 🎶
**📅 January 8 – January 14, 2027**

---

### 🎯 What This Week Is About

Every rhyme she knows is a machine for keeping time, and this week she takes
them apart. She fills five shakers and lines them up quietest to loudest, drops
an egg off a wall she built herself, counts five ducks down to none and back
again, and hits an upturned saucepan once for every piece of a word. The rhymes
stay the same all week. Her job with them changes each morning.

No holiday lands in this stretch, so the week runs on its own thread and the
gear piles up as it goes. Five sealed shakers on Friday, a kitchen drum kit on
Monday, a hat and a flag on Thursday, and by the last afternoon the whole house
is marching a route Azlyn drew on a sheet of paper on the floor. Three
afternoons end with your phone recording her singing, one of them a song she
writes about Kreston. Back those up before Thursday.

---

### 🧒 What Azlyn Will Learn

- **The beat inside a word.** AZ-LYN is two hits, SPA-GHET-TI is four, and she hears the pieces long before she can name them.
- **Loud and quiet on purpose.** Scarf up is loud, scarf down is quiet, and she is the one holding the scarf.
- **Counting down, and starting again from one.** Five ducks to none, ten in the bed to one, five buns off a tray.
- **Calling it before it happens.** She says cracked or not cracked out loud, then opens her hand over the tray and finds out.
- **Patterns she can hear.** Spoon, cup, spoon, cup, and the gap where you took one out of the middle.

---

### 👩 What You'll Learn

- Why she only has one volume, and it is loud
- Why the rhymes she loves are all a bit gruesome
- Why she wants the same rhyme forty times
- Rhythm and reading are more connected than they look
- Why a wind-down object works better than a wind-down instruction
- Counting words come long before counting means anything
- Why moving together matters more than moving

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Listening to the Cupboard

**🧰 You need:** the dry-goods cupboard, open · 1 metal spoon · 5 empty pots with lids.

1. Sit her in front of the open cupboard and let her tap the packets with the back of the spoon. Rice, pasta, lentils, cereal, sugar. Each answers in a different voice.
2. Shut your eyes and let her tap one for you to name. Get one wrong on purpose.
3. Line up the five pots and let her decide which packet goes in which. You label them at 9:15.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Shaker Set

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 5 small plastic bottles or yoghurt pots with lids
- Dried rice, 1 cup
- Dried lentils, 1 cup
- Dried pasta shapes, 1 cup
- Sugar, 2 tablespoons
- Dry cereal, a handful
- Parcel tape
- 1 permanent marker
- 1 sheet of paper rolled into a funnel

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Roll the paper into a cone, tape the seam and stand it in the first pot. She pours, you hold. Some goes on the table and that is the price.
2. Fill each pot a third full. A pot packed to the lid makes almost no sound, so let her overfill one and hear it.
3. Tape every lid all the way round twice and press the seams flat. Write the contents on the side of each pot in marker.
4. Shake them one at a time with her eyes shut. Ask her to point at the loudest before she opens them.
5. Stand all five along the table, quietest to loudest. Move them as often as she wants.
6. Number the bases one to five once she settles. She comes back to this line at 4:15.

> 💡 **Tip:** The sugar shaker sounds like rain and she will lose interest inside a minute. Keep it. It is the quiet end of the line, and the only instrument in this afternoon's whisper verse.

### 🌳 Get Outside: Tapping the Railings

Take a wooden spoon out with you and tap what you pass. Railings ring and hold
the note, fence posts thud and stop dead, a wheelie bin booms, a brick wall
gives back nothing. January metal is cold enough that she will want gloves on
and then off again to feel it. Let her find the loudest thing on the street and
stay at it.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 7 months and reaching across his own
> body for things on the far side. Sit him back from the table with a wooden
> spoon and an upturned saucepan, and put the spoon down on his far side so he
> has to cross over for it. Keep the small pots away from him. Shake one by his
> left ear, then his right, and watch him turn toward a sound he cannot see.

<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>

### 🎨 Second Main Event: Loud Song, Quiet Song

The shakers have their order. This afternoon Azlyn plays one rhyme four ways
and decides which way comes next.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The five sealed shakers
- 1 saucepan and 1 wooden spoon
- 1 cardboard tube for a megaphone
- 1 scarf as the conductor's signal
- Your phone, to record the last run

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Sit everyone in a ring, shakers in the middle, saucepan by your knee, scarf in Azlyn's hand.
2. Pick a rhyme the whole house knows. Twinkle, Twinkle or The Wheels on the Bus. Sing it through once with a shaker each.
3. Loud version. Saucepan on the beat, cardboard tube as a megaphone, pasta and lentils flat out. One verse, and one verse alone.
4. Quiet version. Sugar shaker on its own, everyone whispering, heads close together. She drops her voice to match yours.
5. Fast, then slow. Slow is the hard one, and one verse at half speed lasts longer than either of you expects.
6. Hand her the scarf. Scarf up is loud, scarf down is quiet, and she runs the band. Follow her even when she flips it every two words.
7. Record the last run. Play it back once, then again with everyone singing on top of it.

> 💡 **Tip:** The whisper verse works better if you get smaller. Sit on the floor, lean in, let your voice go thin.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Two shakers and one rhyme, sat on the sofa.
- March round the kitchen table with the saucepan and nothing else.
- Sort the shakers by what is inside instead of how loud they are.
- Sing the same rhyme in a squeaky voice, a growly voice and a whisper.

### 🌳 Out Again: **Pick one**

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** He is 7 months and babbling in long strings now,
> ba-ba-ba and da-da-da-da, a rhythm section of his own. Sit him in the ring
> with a folded blanket behind him. He answers singing sooner than talking, so
> leave a gap at the end of each verse and see whether he fills it. Keep the
> loud verse short and watch his face while it runs.

### 🌍 Around the World: Lullabies Everywhere 🌙

Every country puts its children to sleep with a song, and the songs resemble
each other more than the languages do. Wales has *Suo Gân*, sung on the same
slow fall as the Japanese *Itsuki no Komoriuta*. Swahili families sing *Lala
salama*, sleep safe. Researchers played babies lullabies from cultures they had
never heard, and the babies settled to those too.

> **✨ Try it:** Warm milk with a cinnamon stick, the bedtime drink in a great
> many houses. Give Azlyn hers in the same cup all week and sing one lullaby
> that is not in English.

> *Did you know?* Lullabies the world over share a slow, falling tune, which is
> why a song in a language she has never met still works on her.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Why she only has one volume, and it is loud.**
> Azlyn can do quiet on request and hold it for about four seconds. The holding
> is the hard part, and it runs on the same slow-growing machinery as waiting
> her turn and stopping halfway down the hall. Volume control is inhibition,
> and a three-year-old has a thin supply. She gets better at it by practicing
> the switch rather than by being asked for less noise. Handing her the loud
> verse first and the whisper verse second gives her both ends to feel in her
> own throat a minute apart. The scarf makes her the one calling the change,
> and running the switch builds it faster than obeying it.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Dried rice, lentils and pasta on the floor are a choking
> hazard for a 7-month-old, and a taped lid can still come off. Tape all the way
> round each pot, check every seal before it goes down, and keep the small pots
> away from Kreston.

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Drawing on the Eggs

**🧰 You need:** 4 cold boiled eggs · 1 permanent marker · 1 egg cup.

1. Hand her the marker and one egg at a time. Faces, hats, whatever she wants. Marker takes a minute to dry on a shell, so hold each one by the ends.
2. Say the rhyme together while she draws. She will reach the line about nobody putting him together again and look up at you.
3. Stand the best one in the egg cup on the windowsill. Nobody touches him before 9:15.

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Egg Drop Record

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 4 hard-boiled eggs, boiled last night and cooled
- 1 baking tray
- 1 old towel to line the tray
- 6–8 books or small boxes to build the wall
- 1 permanent marker
- Paper for the record
- 1 tape measure

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Line the tray with the folded towel and set it on the kitchen floor. She stacks the books into a wall beside it.
2. Sit Humpty on top and say the rhyme once with him up there.
3. First drop from ankle height, straight into the tray. Measure with the tape and write the number down before she opens her hand.
4. Ask her to call it before every drop: cracked or not cracked. Write her answer beside the number. Knee height next, then hip.
5. Take the towel out for the last egg and drop it on the bare tray from ankle height. It breaks where it was safe five minutes ago.
6. Read the paper back to her. Her numbers, her guesses, and the one that caught her out.

> 💡 **Tip:** Let her hold the egg and let her be the one who opens her hand. An egg she drops herself is her experiment.

### 🌳 Get Outside: The Low Wall Balance

Find a low garden wall, a curb edge or the flat top of a step and walk her
along it holding one hand. She sits at the far end with her legs over and says
the rhyme from up there, then jumps into your hands. January stone keeps its
frost all morning in the shade, so put your own hand flat on the top first.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 7 months and has started dropping food
> over the side of the high chair and leaning out to look. Same experiment,
> cheaper equipment. Sit him where he can see the tray for three or four drops.
> Then give him an egg cup on his own tray and pick it up eight times without
> comment.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Mended Egg

Humpty's shell is still sitting on the tray. She glues him back together this
afternoon, the one thing the rhyme insists nobody could do.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- The cracked shells from this morning
- 1 sheet of thick paper or card
- A glue stick, or flour and water paste in a saucer
- 1 teaspoon for the paste
- Crayons
- 1 pencil to draw the egg outline

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Draw a big egg outline on the card in pencil, or let her draw it and go over her line so she can see it.
2. Tip the shell onto a sheet of paper and let her press it small with the back of a spoon. Fingers work too.
3. Mix the paste if you are using it: two tablespoons of flour, water stirred in until it runs like cream.
4. Cover a small patch of the egg at a time. Paste the whole outline at once and it dries before she has filled a third of it.
5. She presses the shell down. Let her go outside the line and leave gaps, and keep your hands off.
6. Crayon in the rest: the wall underneath, the king's horses, a face on the shell.
7. Write her title along the bottom in her own words, then put it up high to dry.

> 💡 **Tip:** The shell runs out near the top and she will notice. Crayon fills the gap, and the picture is better for one bald patch.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives
- Shells into a bowl of water and stir. That is the whole activity.
- Draw Humpty on a flattened box and glue nothing.
- Paint the shells today and glue them tomorrow.
- Sort the pieces into big, medium and tiny on three saucers.

### 🌳 Out Again: **Pick one**
- **A bus ride to nowhere in particular**, upstairs at the front
- The park, for the low wall she balanced on this morning
- Soft play if the weather has gone sideways
- A walk to look at real brick walls and count the courses
- The shop for eggs, since you are four down

> 👶 **Kreston's Afternoon:** Keep him at the table and off the shells. Stick a
> long strip of masking tape to his high chair tray and let him work it loose.
> At 7 months he moves a thing from one hand to the other for a better angle.
> On the bus he wants the window, so face him out on your lap and name the
> stops.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Why the rhymes she loves are all a bit gruesome.**
> Humpty smashes. The cradle falls out of the tree, baby and all. Three mice
> lose their tails to a carving knife. Azlyn asks for these on repeat and then
> sleeps fine, because a rhyme keeps a small disaster inside a shape that never
> alters. She knows the ending before it arrives, so the fall frightens her the
> way a slide frightens her, with the floor already in view. Rehearsing an
> alarming thing at a safe distance is how she works out its real size.
> Dropping the egg herself went further again: she caused the crack, she stood
> over it, and it turned out smaller than the rhyme makes it sound.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Shell fragments have sharp edges and are exactly the size
> Kreston puts in his mouth. Keep the tray and the glued sheet up high, sweep
> the floor before he goes down, and wipe egg off a hard floor straight away
> because it is slippery.

### 🌅 Opening Activity: Making the Five Ducks

**🧰 You need:** 1 flattened cereal box · scissors (adult only) · crayons · 5 clothes pegs.

1. Draw five duck shapes on the plain side of the box. Rough ovals with a beak on the front will do.
2. You cut, she colors. Give each one something different, and let her choose which is the mother duck.
3. Clip a peg to the bottom of each so they stand up. Line all five along the table and count them twice.

### 🎨 The Main Event: Over the Hill and Far Away

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 cereal box, for cutting five duck shapes
- Scissors (adult only)
- Crayons
- 5 clothes pegs to stand the ducks up
- 1 upturned laundry basket or a sofa cushion
- 1 blue dish towel or scarf for the pond
- Masking tape

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Turn the laundry basket upside down in the middle of the floor for the hill. Spread the dish towel in front of it for the pond and tape the corners flat.
2. Stand all five ducks on the pond. Run a strip of masking tape across the floor behind the hill to mark where far away starts.
3. Sing the first verse. On the line about only four coming back, she takes one duck over the hill and stands it on the tape.
4. Count what is left on the pond after each verse, touching each duck as its number comes. She does the quacking, all of it.
5. Keep going down to none, and sit in the quiet of an empty pond before the last verse.
6. Mother duck calls and all five come back over together. She carries them, and she will want the whole thing again, so do it again.

> 💡 **Tip:** Put the vanished ducks somewhere she can see the tops of. A duck gone for good gets checked on halfway through the next verse.

### 🌳 Get Outside: Walking in a Duck Line

Walk the block in single file. She goes first as the mother duck, everyone
behind her waddles, and at each corner the person at the back moves to the
front. Count off before you set out and count again at home to see whether
anybody went over the hill and far away. January puddles have ice at the rim
and she will stamp every one of them.

> 👶 **Infant Integration:** Kreston is 7 months. Prop him with a cushion on

> and drops out of sight. Gone and back is the funniest thing available to him

> gap between verses.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Rhyme Bag

One object per rhyme goes into a pillowcase, and the bag decides what the house
sings.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 1 pillowcase or tote bag
- 6–8 household objects, one per rhyme: a spoon, a card star, a rolled sock, a watch, a plastic cup, an egg cup, one of the cardboard ducks
- 1 sheet of card and 1 marker for picture labels

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Gather the objects and give each one its rhyme out loud. The spoon ran away with the dish, the mouse ran up the watch, the sock is Diddle Diddle Dumpling.
2. Draw a picture label on card for each one, rough and fast, and lay it beside its object.
3. Everything into the pillowcase. She reaches in without looking, feels about, pulls one out.
4. Whatever comes out, the whole house sings it. No skipping and no swapping.
5. Let her guess by feel before she looks, one guess out loud.
6. Once the bag is empty, lay the objects in the order they arrived and sing the set through as one medley.
7. Refill it and hand her the bag. She runs it and you sing whatever she pulls.

> 💡 **Tip:** Slip in one object with no rhyme attached, a shoe or an apple, and let her invent something when it comes out. Her version goes in the bag with the rest.

### 🎯 Afternoon Alternatives

- Line the objects up and let her set the order you sing them in.
- Hide the five ducks round the room and sing a verse for each one found.
- Draw the labels and stop there; the bag can wait until tomorrow.

---

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Why she wants the same rhyme forty times.**
> The fortieth Five Little Ducks is not the same event for her that it is for
> you. The first few passes take everything she has to follow the tune. Once
> the tune runs on its own, she has room to hear the words. Once the words are
> free, she notices the number dropping by one every verse, and after that she
> finds the joke in the last line. Repetition buys her the spare capacity to
> attend to the next layer down. Research on toddler word learning keeps
> landing in the same place: a few books read over and over beat a wide
> rotation for what stays. Sing it again.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** January pond water is cold enough to matter and the edge is
> muddy and steep. Hold her hand at the water, throw the food from well back,
> and stay between both children and the bank. Geese take fingers with the
> bread. The pegs and the small rhyme-bag objects are choke-sized, so they come
> off the floor before Kreston does.

### 🌅 Opening Activity: The Spoon and the Saucepan

### 🎨 The Main Event: The Kitchen Drum Kit

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**

- 1 plastic tub with a lid
- 1 cereal box
- 1 saucepan
- Parcel tape
- 2 wooden spoons

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Line the tin, the tub, the cereal box and the saucepan along the floor and tape their bases down so they stop traveling.

4. Move on to food for longer words. BREAD is one hit. BA-NA-NA is three. SPA-GHET-TI is four and she will want that one over and over.

### 🎨 Second Main Event: The Pattern You Can Hear

She lays a row of objects along the floor, gives each one a sound, and plays
the row left to right.

**🧰 Complete Materials List:**
- 8 spoons
- 8 plastic or paper cups
- 8 socks
- 1 long strip of paper, or a line of masking tape on the floor
- 1 marker
- The drum kit from this morning

**📝 Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Run the masking tape in a long line down the floor, or lay the paper strip out. That is the row.
2. Start it off yourself. Spoon, cup, spoon, cup, spoon, cup, saying each one aloud. Ask her what comes next and wait through the silence.
3. Give every object a sound. Spoon is a tap on the tin, cup is a slap on the tub, sock is silence. She will fill the row with socks.
4. Play the row left to right, pointing at each object as its sound comes. Then again, faster. Then again with her pointing and you playing.
5. Hand the row over. She builds the next one and you play it back, note for note, including the row that is seven socks and a spoon.
6. Take one object out of the middle of a repeating row and play it again. Ask her what went missing.
7. Photograph the last row before you clear it up, then let her play it from the photo.

> 💡 **Tip:** Leave her patterns alone. A row that breaks its own rule is a row she made a decision about.

> 🧠 **A Little Parenting Insight**
> **Rhythm and reading are more connected than they look.**
> Clapping BA-NA-NA into three pieces is reading practice, three years before
> she meets a letter. Reading asks her to hear that a word breaks into parts,
> hold those parts in order, and match them onto shapes on a page. The hearing
> comes first and beat is how children get at it. Preschoolers who can keep
> time with a steady pulse score higher on the sound-awareness tests that
> predict later reading, and the link survives once you account for age and
> vocabulary. Every rhyme sung in this house since Friday has run that
> machinery. The drums make it loud enough for her to catch herself doing it.

> ⚠️ **Safety:** A saucepan banged at close range is loud enough to hurt a
> 7-month-old's ears. Keep Kreston an arm's length back from the kit, give him
> his own quiet tub, and stop the session before it turns into hitting for
> volume. Wooden spoons swing at exactly his head height.
